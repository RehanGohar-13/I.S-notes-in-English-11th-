In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.

Chapter 4(A): Collective Benevolence & Respect for Humanity

Khair Khwahi, Ihtiram-e-Insaniyat and the Prophetic model of social welfare

🎯 Learning Outcomes

After studying this lesson, students will be able to:

  • Understand the meaning and concept of collective benevolence and respect for humanity.
  • Understand the importance and virtue of collective benevolence and respect for humanity in light of Quran and Sunnah, and avoid prevalent forms of degrading humanity.
  • Understand examples of collective benevolence from the Seerat-e-Nabawi ﷺ, Khutbah Hajjat-ul-Wida, the Seerat of Ahl-e-Bait and the Sahaba (RA).
  • Review the social benefits and fruits of collective benevolence and respect for humanity in practical life.
  • Learn from the examples of the Seerat and make them part of their own lives.
  • Play a role in social welfare by participating in acts of collective benevolence in practical life.

Collective Benevolence (Ijtima'i Khair Khwahi)

Collective Benevolence: Having good wishes for all of Allah's creation and thinking well of them.

All Prophets (AS) were well-wishers of their nations. Prophet Hud (AS) addressed his people:

"میں تمھیں اپنے رب کے پیغامات پہنچاتا ہوں اور میں تمھارے لیے امانت دار خیرخواہ ہوں۔"
"I convey to you the messages of my Lord, and I am for you a trustworthy adviser."
— Surah Al-A'raf 7:68

🐜 The Ant of Surah An-Naml

A Surah of the Quran is named "Surah An-Naml" (The Ant). It is named after the ant who warned the other ants when the army of Prophet Sulaiman (AS) approached:

"اے چیونٹیو! اپنے بلوں میں داخل ہو جاؤ سلیمان (علیہ السلام) اور ان کا لشکر تمھیں کچل نہ دیں اور ان کو خبر بھی نہ ہو۔"
"O ants! Enter your dwellings lest Sulaiman and his army crush you without perceiving it."
— Surah An-Naml 27:18

Benefiting people is such a great deed that Allah grants eternal life to such a person. The Quran says:

وَاَمَّا مَا يَنْفَعُ النَّاسَ فَيَمْكُثُ فِي الْاَرْضِ
"And that which benefits the people remains on the earth."
— Surah Ar-Ra'd 13:17
الدِّيْنُ النَّصِيْحَةُ
"The Deen (religion) is the name of sincere well-wishing (Naseehah)."
— Sahih Muslim: 55
• • •

Kindness to the Weak & Vulnerable

The Prophet ﷺ placed special emphasis on kindness and good treatment of the weaker classes of society — women, slaves and orphans.

🕋 Khutbah Hajjat-ul-Wida (Farewell Sermon)

  • Regarding women: "I urge you to fear Allah concerning women. You have taken them in the name of Allah — take care of their rights."
  • Regarding slaves: "Whatever you eat and wear — feed and clothe your slaves with the same."
"بیواؤں اور مسکینوں کے کام آنے والا اللہ کے راستے میں جہاد کرنے والے کے برابر ہے یا رات بھر عبادت اور دن کو روزے رکھنے والے کے برابر ہے۔"
"The one who strives to help widows and the poor is like the one who fights in the path of Allah, or like the one who prays all night and fasts all day."
— Sahih Bukhari: 5353

🐾 Kindness to Animals

The Sahaba (RA) asked: "O Messenger of Allah! Is there reward for us in treating animals well?" The Prophet ﷺ replied:

"Yes, there is a reward in treating every living creature well."
— Sahih Bukhari: 2363

Respect for Humanity (Ihtiram-e-Insaniyat)

Respect for Humanity: "Honouring all human beings." Every person, by virtue of being human, is worthy of respect.
"اور یقیناً ہم نے آدم (علیہ السلام) کی اولاد کو عزت بخشی"
"And indeed, We have honoured the children of Adam."
— Surah Bani Isra'il 17:70

The Quran's address is to all of humanity. Treating all people well is respect for humanity. The Prophet ﷺ said:

"Whoever oppresses a non-Muslim living in the Islamic state, or usurps his right, or burdens him beyond his capacity, or takes anything from him without his willing consent — I will be the advocate of that non-Muslim against the Muslim on the Day of Judgment."
— Sunan Abu Dawood: 3052

⚠️ Sanctity of a Non-Muslim's Life

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever kills a person under covenant (Mu'ahid), he will not even smell the fragrance of Paradise."

— Sahih Bukhari: 3166
• • •

Examples from the Sahaba & Ahl-e-Bait

🤲 Abu Bakr Siddiq (RA)

When Abu Bakr (RA) was elected Caliph, a slave girl who tended goats became worried: "Who will milk our goats now?" Abu Bakr (RA) said:

"By Allah! I will milk them. I hope that the responsibility of the Caliphate will not prevent me from serving Allah's creation."

He also personally served a blind old woman in the outskirts of Madinah throughout his Caliphate.

⚖️ Umar Farooq (RA)

Umar (RA) had such a deep sense of responsibility and accountability that he used to say:

"If a goat dies on the banks of the Euphrates, I will be questioned about it on the Day of Judgment."

💕 Hasan & Husain (RA)

It was the practice of Hasan (RA) and Husain (RA) that whenever they went out to eat, they would invite any poor or needy person to share their meal and eat together with them. Once, some poor people invited Imam Husain (RA) to a meal — he accepted their invitation to encourage them and show love, and ate at their place.

• • •

Forms of Degrading Humanity (Tahqeer-e-Insaniyat)

The following are against the respect for humanity and must be avoided:

  • 🚫 Considering others inferior and looking down on them.
  • 🚫 Mocking and making fun of others.
  • 🚫 Rebuking and dismissing people rudely.
  • 🚫 Distorting names and calling people by offensive nicknames.
  • 🚫 Judging a person's worth based on caste, race or colour.
  • 🚫 Mocking someone's appearance or physical condition.
  • 🚫 Backbiting (Gheebat), taunting and sarcasm.
  • 🚫 Depriving someone of their rights or snatching their rights.
  • 🚫 Mocking someone's illness — instead, we are taught to visit and serve the sick.
  • 🚫 Mocking someone's financial status, appearance, race or any flaw — even in the name of humour and jokes.

Every Muslim should guard the rights of others as demonstrated by the Seerat-e-Tayyibah ﷺ.

• • •

Benefits & Fruits

  • ✅ Brotherhood, love and unity are promoted.
  • ✅ Tolerance is developed in society — because intolerance is a deadly poison.
  • ✅ People receive their rights and everyone's economic condition improves.
  • ✅ Time and money are saved from being wasted on fights and lawsuits.
  • ✅ Hatred is eliminated and a spirit of cooperation is born.
  • ✅ Everyone's life, wealth and honour are protected.

Question 2: Short Answers

(i) Write the concept of collective benevolence. ▼

Collective benevolence (Ijtima'i Khair Khwahi) means having good wishes for all of Allah's creation and thinking well of them. It is the essence of the Deen, as the Prophet ﷺ said: "Ad-Deen An-Naseehah — The religion is the name of sincere well-wishing" (Muslim: 55). All Prophets (AS) were well-wishers of their nations, and the Quran praises those whose actions benefit humanity: "That which benefits the people remains on the earth" (Ar-Ra'd 13:17). It extends to all living creatures — the Prophet ﷺ confirmed that there is reward in treating every living being well (Bukhari: 2363).

(ii) State the concept of respect for humanity. ▼

Respect for humanity (Ihtiram-e-Insaniyat) means honouring all human beings. Every person, by virtue of being human, is worthy of respect. The Quran declares: "Indeed, We have honoured the children of Adam" (Bani Isra'il 17:70). The Quran's address is to all of humanity, not just Muslims. The Prophet ﷺ guaranteed the rights of non-Muslim citizens, declaring he would be their advocate against any Muslim oppressor on the Day of Judgment (Abu Dawood: 3052). He ﷺ also warned that killing a person under covenant means one will not even smell the fragrance of Paradise (Bukhari: 3166).

(iii) Write one example of collective benevolence from the life of Abu Bakr Siddiq (RA). ▼

When Abu Bakr (RA) was elected as the first Caliph, a slave girl who tended goats became worried and asked: "Who will milk our goats now?" When Abu Bakr (RA) learned of this, he said: "By Allah! I will milk them. I hope that the responsibility of the Caliphate will not prevent me from serving Allah's creation." He continued to personally milk the goats even as the head of the entire Muslim state. Additionally, he personally served a blind old woman in the outskirts of Madinah throughout his Caliphate — a powerful example that leadership means service, not privilege.

(iv) Write two prevalent forms of degrading humanity. ▼
  1. Mockery and ridicule: Making fun of someone's appearance, physical condition, financial status, race or colour — even in the name of humour and jokes. This is a grave sin and unacceptable in any civilised society.
  2. Backbiting (Gheebat) and taunting: Speaking ill of someone behind their back, taunting them about their flaws or circumstances, or using sarcasm to belittle them.
(v) Write two benefits of collective benevolence and respect for humanity. ▼
  1. Promotion of brotherhood, love and unity: When people genuinely wish well for each other and respect one another, bonds of brotherhood and love are strengthened, creating a cohesive and harmonious society.
  2. Protection of life, wealth and honour: When the feelings of benevolence and respect flourish in society, everyone's life, property and dignity are safeguarded — eliminating violence, theft and oppression.

Question 3: Detailed Answer

Collective benevolence and respect for humanity are the guarantors of social welfare — explain. ▼

Introduction

Islam places extraordinary emphasis on collective benevolence (Ijtima'i Khair Khwahi) and respect for humanity (Ihtiram-e-Insaniyat). These two principles are the foundation of a peaceful, just and prosperous society. The Prophet ﷺ declared: "Ad-Deen An-Naseehah — The religion is the name of sincere well-wishing" (Muslim: 55).

1. Quranic Foundation

All Prophets (AS) were well-wishers of their nations. Hud (AS) said: "I am for you a trustworthy adviser" (Al-A'raf 7:68). The Quran declares that "that which benefits the people remains on the earth" (Ar-Ra'd 13:17). Allah honoured all of humanity: "Indeed, We have honoured the children of Adam" (Bani Isra'il 17:70).

2. Prophetic Model

  • In the Khutbah Hajjat-ul-Wida, he ﷺ urged the Ummah to fear Allah regarding women's rights and to feed and clothe slaves as they feed and clothe themselves.
  • He ﷺ declared that helping widows and the poor is equal to Jihad or praying all night and fasting all day (Bukhari: 5353).
  • He ﷺ confirmed that there is reward in treating every living creature well (Bukhari: 2363).
  • He ﷺ guaranteed the rights of non-Muslim citizens, declaring himself their advocate against any Muslim oppressor (Abu Dawood: 3052).
  • He ﷺ warned that killing a person under covenant means one will not smell Paradise (Bukhari: 3166).

3. Examples from the Sahaba & Ahl-e-Bait

Abu Bakr (RA) continued milking a slave girl's goats even after becoming Caliph, and personally served a blind old woman. Umar (RA) declared he would be accountable for even a goat dying on the banks of the Euphrates. Hasan and Husain (RA) regularly invited the poor to share their meals and accepted invitations from the needy to encourage them.

4. Avoiding Degradation of Humanity

Islam forbids all forms of degrading humanity: mockery, backbiting, racism, caste-based discrimination, taunting, nicknaming, and ridiculing someone's illness, appearance or financial status. Even humour is not an excuse for belittling others.

5. Social Benefits

  • Brotherhood, love and unity are promoted.
  • Tolerance replaces intolerance.
  • People receive their rights and economic conditions improve.
  • Time and money are saved from fights and lawsuits.
  • Hatred is eliminated and cooperation flourishes.
  • Everyone's life, wealth and honour are protected.

Conclusion

Collective benevolence and respect for humanity are not merely moral ideals — they are the guarantors of social welfare. When every member of society genuinely wishes well for others and honours their dignity, the result is a peaceful, just and prosperous community — the very model the Prophet ﷺ established in Madinah.
