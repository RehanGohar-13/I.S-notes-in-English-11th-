Sawm (Roza) — The Philosophy of Fasting

Chapter 2(C) — Ibadaat (Acts of Worship) · Philosophy of Fasting · Textbook pp. 47–50

بِسْمِ اللهِ الرَّحْمٰنِ الرَّحِيْمِ "In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful."

🎯 Learning Outcomes

After studying this lesson, students will be able to:

  1. Understand the philosophy of fasting across religions, its obligation in Islam, virtues, rulings (ada, qadha, kaffarah, fidya), and types of fasts.
  2. Become aware of the wisdoms of the Sunnah acts of Ramadan — Qiyam-ul-Layl, abundant charity, Quran recitation, masnoon du'as, I'tikaf, Laylat-ul-Qadr, and Fitrana.
  3. Evaluate the effects of fasting on practical life.
  4. Fast with proper etiquette to cultivate qualities like Taqwa and Sabr.
  5. Make arrangements for the Sunnah acts of Ramadan — Quran recitation, I'tikaf, charity, and Fitrana.

📖 The Meaning of Sawm (Fasting)

In the Quran, fasting is called Sawm. The literal meaning of Sawm is "to hold back / to refrain."

In Islamic terminology, Sawm means to refrain from eating, drinking, and certain other lawful desires — for the pleasure of Allah ﷻ — from Subh-e-Sadiq (true dawn) until sunset.

⚖️ The Obligation & Importance of Fasting

Fasting is an important pillar of Islam. Allah ﷻ has made the fasts of Ramadan obligatory upon Muslims:

يٰٓاَيُّهَا الَّذِيْنَ اٰمَنُوْا كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الصِّيَامُ كَمَا كُتِبَ عَلَي الَّذِيْنَ مِنْ قَبْلِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُوْنَ "O you who believe! Fasting has been prescribed upon you as it was prescribed upon those before you, so that you may attain Taqwa (be saved from disobedience)." Surah Al-Baqarah 2:183

The Prophet ﷺ highlighted the immense reward of fasting Ramadan:

"Whoever fasts the month of Ramadan with faith (iman) and self-accountability (ihtisab) — his previous sins will be forgiven." Sahih Bukhari: 1901

The Quran clearly states that fasting was also obligatory upon previous nations. Even today, followers of various religions observe fasts — though the nature and timing of their fasts differ from those of Muslims.
· · ·

📋 Rulings & Issues of Fasting

The fasts of Ramadan are obligatory on every sane, mature, healthy, and resident Muslim.

Concessions & Compensations

Situation Ruling
Sick or Traveller May skip the fast; must perform qadha later.
Permanently Ill (no hope of recovery) Must pay fidya for each missed fast.
Deliberately Breaking a Ramadan Fast Both qadha + kaffarah are obligatory.

🍲 Fidya

Feeding one miskeen two meals (morning and evening) for each missed fast.

⛓️ Kaffarah

Fasting 60 consecutive days plus 1 qadha fast. If even one day is missed in between, the 60 must restart from scratch.

If physically impossible → feed 60 miskeens (either all in one day, or one miskeen for 60 days).

🗂️ Types of Fasts

1️⃣ Fardh Mu'ayyan

(Fixed Obligatory)

A fast whose timing is fixed and specified.

  • Fasts of Ramadan
  • Fast of a specific vow (Nazr-e-Mu'ayyan)

2️⃣ Fardh Ghayr Mu'ayyan

(Unfixed Obligatory)

A fast that is obligatory but has no fixed time.

  • Fasts of Kaffarah
  • Fasts of Qadha
  • Fast of an unspecified vow (Nazr-e-Ghayr Mu'ayyan)

3️⃣ Nafl

(Voluntary)

  • Fast of 9 Dhul-Hijjah (Yawm-e-Arafah)
  • Fasts of 9th & 10th Muharram

🌙 Sunnah Acts of Ramadan al-Mubarak

Among the Islamic months, Ramadan is the most virtuous. Allah ﷻ multiplies the reward of good deeds up to 70 times in this blessed month.

The Masnoon Du'a of Laylat-ul-Qadr

Hazrat Aisha Siddiqa (RA) asked the Prophet ﷺ: "O Messenger of Allah! If I find Laylat-ul-Qadr, what du'a should I make?" The Prophet ﷺ taught her:

اَللّٰهُمَّ اِنَّكَ عَفُوٌّ كَرِيْمٌ تُحِبُّ الْعَفْوَ فَاعْفُ عَنِّيْ "O Allah! You are the One who pardons much and loves pardoning, so pardon me." Jami' Tirmidhi: 3513

Taraweeh & Quran Recitation

  • After Isha prayer, Taraweeh is offered — in which the full Quran is recited.
  • Taraweeh is also called Qiyam-ul-Layl.
  • Ramadan and the Quran have a deep connection — the Quran was revealed in this month.
  • The Prophet ﷺ used to recite the Quran abundantly in Ramadan, and would review the Quran with Hazrat Jibreel (AS).
  • In his final Ramadan, the Prophet ﷺ reviewed the Quran with Jibreel (AS) twice.

The Last 10 Days — I'tikaf & Laylat-ul-Qadr

The Prophet ﷺ worshipped Allah ﷻ throughout the year, but in the last ten days of Ramadan he would make extraordinary arrangements for worship and would also instruct his family to engage in Allah's worship.

  • 🕌 I'tikaf in the last 10 days of Ramadan is a Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ.
  • 🌟 Laylat-ul-Qadr should be sought in these days — in one of the odd nights of the last ten.
  • ✨ It is a night of immense blessings and goodness.
لَيْلَةُ الْقَدْرِ ۬ خَيْرٌ مِّنْ اَلْفِ شَهْرٍ "The Night of Decree is better than a thousand months." Surah Al-Qadr 97:3

Sadaqat-ul-Fitr (Fitrana)

Upon the completion of Ramadan, Sadaqat-ul-Fitr is paid before the Eid Salah.

  • Wajib on every Sahib-e-Nisab Muslim for himself and his minor children.
  • It is a thanksgiving for completing Ramadan.
  • Through it, the needy also share in the joys of Eid.

💫 Effects of Fasting on Practical Life

Spiritual & Moral Effects

Fasting produces Taqwa, protects a person from sins, and inclines him toward righteousness. The Prophet ﷺ said:

"Fasting is a shield against sins." Sahih Bukhari: 7492

Ramadan is the month of peace and reconciliation. The Prophet ﷺ instructed:

"If someone wishes to quarrel with you, say to him: 'I am fasting.'" Sahih Bukhari: 1894

Social Effects

  • 👥 Rich & poor, master & servant, ruler & ruled all fast for a continuous month → sense of equality (musawat) develops.
  • 🕰️ Shared times of Suhoor and Iftar create harmony in society.
  • ⚖️ The gap between rich and poor begins to dissolve.
  • 🥖 When a fasting person feels even the simplest food delicious at Iftar — simplicity and contentment (qana'at) are cultivated.
  • 🤲 The du'as of a fasting person are accepted.
  • 💚 Allah's pleasure is attained, and the person's health is maintained.

Time Discipline

The fixed times of Suhoor and Iftar make the fasting person punctual with time. This care about time produces discipline (nazm-o-zabt) in life. One month of this practice trains the believer to perform all other matters of life on time as well.

Physical & Health Effects

Physicians across the world agree that a constantly full stomach gives birth to many diseases. When the stomach is empty during fasting:

  • 🛡️ The body is protected from many diseases.
  • 💪 Immunity (quwwat-e-mudafiat) develops in the body's organs.
  • 🧹 The body is cleansed of excess fluids and toxins.
  • 🧠 Important for students — intelligence and memory are linked to balanced diet and a healthy stomach.
  • 🍽️ Fasting plays a key role in correcting the digestive system.
Fasting is the best system of training (tarbiyat). It contains all aspects of moral and habitual reform. Through fasting, our body stays healthy and our soul is purified.

Conclusion: We should make full arrangements for the fasts of Ramadan so that we may be freed from spiritual and physical diseases and attain success in this world and the Hereafter.

Q2. Short Questions

(i) Write the meaning and definition of Sawm (fasting). ▼

Literal meaning: The word Sawm means "to hold back / to refrain."

Islamic definition: Sawm means to refrain from eating, drinking, and certain other lawful desires — for the pleasure of Allah ﷻ — from Subh-e-Sadiq (true dawn) until sunset. It is made obligatory on believers so that they may attain Taqwa (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:183).

(ii) Write about the Fidya of fasting. ▼

Fidya is the compensation given for a missed fast when a person is permanently ill and has no hope of recovery (and therefore cannot perform qadha).

The fidya for each missed fast is to feed one miskeen (needy person) two meals — morning and evening. Alternatively, the equivalent amount of grain (equal to Sadaqat-ul-Fitr) may be given.

(iii) Why is the fast of Ramadan called a Fardh Mu'ayyan fast? ▼

A Fardh Mu'ayyan fast is one which is both obligatory and whose time of performance is fixed and specified.

The fasts of Ramadan fall into this category because:

  1. Allah ﷻ has made them fardh upon every sane, mature, healthy, resident Muslim (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:183).
  2. Their time is specifically fixed — the month of Ramadan al-Mubarak.

Hence they are Fardh Mu'ayyan, like the fast of a specific vow (Nazr-e-Mu'ayyan).

(iv) Write any two Sunnah acts of the month of Ramadan. ▼

1. Taraweeh (Qiyam-ul-Layl): Offering Taraweeh after Isha prayer, in which the full Quran is recited. The Prophet ﷺ himself engaged in abundant night worship, especially in the last ten days.

2. I'tikaf in the Last Ten Days: Sitting in seclusion in the mosque during the last ten days of Ramadan — a confirmed Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ — while seeking Laylat-ul-Qadr, which the Quran describes as "better than a thousand months" (Surah Al-Qadr 97:3).

(v) Write any two effects of fasting on human health. ▼

1. Protection from Diseases & Building Immunity: Physicians agree that a constantly full stomach causes many illnesses. When the stomach is empty during fasting, the body is protected from many diseases and the organs develop quwwat-e-mudafiat (immunity).

2. Detoxification & Digestive Health: Fasting cleanses the body of excess fluids and toxins, and plays a key role in correcting the digestive system. This is especially important for students, since intelligence and memory are closely linked to balanced diet and digestive health.

Q3. Detailed Question

Write a detailed note on Sawm (fasting) in the light of Islamic teachings. ▼

Introduction: Sawm literally means "to hold back." Islamically, it is to refrain from eating, drinking, and certain lawful desires for Allah's ﷻ pleasure, from true dawn (Subh-e-Sadiq) until sunset. It is a pillar of Islam.

Obligation: Allah ﷻ commands: "O you who believe! Fasting is prescribed upon you as it was prescribed upon those before you, so that you may attain Taqwa" (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:183). The Prophet ﷺ said whoever fasts Ramadan with faith and self-accountability will have his past sins forgiven (Bukhari 1901).

Rulings & Compensations:

  • Sick / Traveller: Qadha later.
  • Permanently Ill: Fidya — feeding one miskeen two meals per missed fast.
  • Deliberately Breaking: Both Qadha + Kaffarah (60 consecutive fasts + 1 qadha; or feeding 60 miskeens if impossible).

Types of Fasts:

  • Fardh Mu'ayyan: Ramadan, specific vow.
  • Fardh Ghayr Mu'ayyan: Kaffarah, Qadha, unspecified vow.
  • Nafl: 9 Dhul-Hijjah, 9th & 10th Muharram.

Sunnah Acts of Ramadan: Taraweeh (Qiyam-ul-Layl), abundant Quran recitation, I'tikaf in the last 10 days, seeking Laylat-ul-Qadr ("better than a thousand months" — Surah Al-Qadr 97:3), the masnoon du'a (Allahumma innaka 'Afuwwun… — Tirmidhi 3513), and Sadaqat-ul-Fitr before Eid Salah.

Effects:

  • Spiritual: Taqwa, shield against sins (Bukhari 7492), avoiding quarrels ("I am fasting" — Bukhari 1894), accepted du'as.
  • Social: Equality between rich and poor, harmony through shared Suhoor and Iftar, simplicity, contentment.
  • Discipline: Punctuality through fixed Suhoor/Iftar times.
  • Health: Protection from disease, immunity, detoxification, improved digestion, mental clarity for students.
Fasting is the best system of training — purifying both body and soul and preparing the believer for success in this world and the Hereafter.
