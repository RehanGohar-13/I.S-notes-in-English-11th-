Sciences of the Quran

Chapter 1(A) — Uloom-ul-Quran · Textbook pp. 1–3

بِسْمِ اللهِ الرَّحْمٰنِ الرَّحِيْمِ "In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful."

🎯 Learning Outcomes

After studying this lesson, students will be able to:

  1. Understand the virtues and characteristics of the Holy Quran — its universality, eternity, comprehensiveness, and perfection.
  2. Know the blessed names of the Quran and their meanings.
  3. Define Makki and Madani Surahs and their characteristics, and understand the basic concept of Ayat-ul-Ahkam (Verses of Rulings).
  4. Strengthen their faith that the Quran is a divine and miraculous book.
  5. Believe in the Quran's miraculous nature, regard it as the final and eternal source of guidance, and act upon its teachings.

📖 What are the "Sciences of the Quran"?

Uloom-ul-Quran (Sciences of the Quran) refers to the body of knowledge that Quranic commentators (Mufassireen) have derived from the subjects and contents of the Holy Quran.

These sciences include:

  • Makki & Madani Surahs — Classification based on place of revelation
  • Muhkamat & Mutashabihat — Clear vs. allegorical verses
  • Shan-e-Nuzool — Reasons and circumstances of revelation
  • Huroof-e-Muqatta'at — The disjointed/mysterious letters (e.g., Alif-Lam-Meem)
  • Nasikh wa Mansukh — Abrogating and abrogated verses
The Holy Quran is the Word of Allah and His final book, revealed to the Last Prophet Muhammad ﷺ over a period of approximately 23 years. It is Allah's message of guidance for all of humanity and the greatest miracle of the Prophet ﷺ, which will remain preserved until the Day of Judgment.

The Quran is the summary and essence of the teachings of all previous heavenly books. Allah has also declared the Quran as the guardian (Muhaimin) over all other divine scriptures. It possesses countless characteristics and is an unparalleled speech in every respect.

⭐ Characteristics of the Holy Quran

The prominent characteristics of the Holy Quran are as follows:

1. Universality (Aalmgeeriyat)

Universality means that the Holy Quran addresses all of humanity — not just a specific nation, region, or race.

Previous heavenly books were revealed for particular communities, but the Quran is a universal book that serves as:

  • A treasure of all kinds of knowledge and wisdom
  • A means to understand the will of Allah

In numerous verses, the Quran addresses all of humanity with the words:

يٰۤاَيُّهَا النَّاسُ "O Mankind!"

It has been explicitly stated that the Quran is a message of guidance and advice for all people. Allah says:

اِنْ هُوَ اِلَّا ذِكْرٌ لِّلْعٰلَمِيْنَ "It is nothing but a reminder for all the worlds." Surah At-Takweer 81:27

And further:

هٰذَا بَلٰغٌ لِّلنَّاسِ "This (Quran) is a message from Allah for all people." Surah Ibrahim 14:52

In short, the teachings of the Holy Quran are for every era, every region, and every race — and this is its universality.

Unlike the Torah (revealed for Bani Israel) or the Injeel (for the followers of Isa AS), the Quran was revealed for every human being regardless of race, language, geography, or era. This makes it the only truly universal divine scripture.
· · ·

2. Completeness / Perfection (Kaamiliyat)

Completeness means that the guidance of the Holy Quran is perfect and complete.

The chain of divine revelation (Wahi) that began with Prophet Adam (AS) came to its conclusion with the Last Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. The religion was perfected upon him ﷺ. The Quran, revealed to him ﷺ, is the most complete form of the teachings of all previous heavenly books.

The Holy Quran is a complete code of life (Ma'kmal Zabitah-e-Hayat). Allah says:

اِنْ هُوَ اِلَّا ذِكْرٌ لِّلْعٰلَمِيْنَ "It is nothing but a reminder for all the worlds." Surah At-Takweer 81:27
The Quran is not a partial or incomplete guide. It is the final and perfected version of all divine guidance sent to humanity since the time of Adam (AS). No further revelation will come after it.
· · ·

3. Comprehensiveness (Jami'iyat)

Comprehensiveness means that the Holy Quran contains complete guidance for all fields and departments of life.

The Quran is a comprehensive book covering every aspect of human existence. Some previous heavenly books contained only moral instructions, some only matters of belief and worship, and some only legal rulings. But the Quran is a comprehensive book that includes:

  • Beliefs (Aqaid)
  • Worship (Ibadat)
  • Dealings & Transactions (Mu'amalat)
  • Social Conduct (Mu'asharat)
  • Morality & Ethics (Akhlaqiyat)
  • Laws & Legislation (Qawaneen)

Allah says:

وَنَزَّلْنَا عَلَيْكَ الْكِتٰبَ تِبْيَانًا لِّكُلِّ شَيْءٍ "And We have revealed to you (O Prophet ﷺ) the Book as a clear explanation of everything." Surah An-Nahl 16:89

In short, the Holy Quran is such a comprehensive book that it contains guidance for the solution of all problems.

· · ·

4. Eternity (Abdiyyat)

Eternity means that the teachings of the Holy Quran are forever and everlasting.

This book of guidance is the source of success in both this world and the Hereafter, and it is the standard (Meezan) for all affairs. The teachings of the Quran are practicable in every era.

The Quran's teachings are so natural and innate (fitri) that people of every age feel as though these teachings were revealed specifically for their guidance. The Quran's teachings are equally beneficial and practicable for all people until the Day of Judgment.

The Quran is not time-bound. Its laws on justice, morality, economics, and human rights are as relevant in the 21st century as they were in 7th-century Arabia. This is the miracle of its eternity.

🕌 Makki and Madani Surahs

The Holy Quran consists of 30 Juz (parts) and 114 Surahs. These Surahs are classified into two categories based on the time and place of their revelation:

🕋 Makki Surahs

The Surahs revealed before the Migration (Hijrah) to Madinah are called Makki Surahs.

Key characteristics:

  • The primary audience was the polytheists of Makkah
  • Focus on Tawheed (Oneness), Risalat (Prophethood), and Akhirat (Hereafter)
  • Emphasis on patience and steadfastness for the Prophet ﷺ and early Muslims
  • Stories of previous nations and their fates
  • Correction of beliefs and reform of morals
  • Logical refutation of idol worship
  • Rich in literary beauty and miraculous eloquence (matching the Arabs' love of rhetoric)

🕌 Madani Surahs

The Surahs revealed after the Migration (Hijrah) to Madinah are called Madani Surahs.

Key characteristics:

  • The Prophet ﷺ had established an Islamic state in Madinah
  • Contains rulings on Jihad, warfare, rights, duties
  • Contains family and civil laws
  • Generally addresses the believers with: "O you who believe!"
  • Style of expression is simple and straightforward
يٰۤاَيُّهَا الَّذِيْنَ اٰمَنُوْا "O you who believe!"

⚖️ Ayat-ul-Ahkam (Verses of Rulings)

Ayat-ul-Ahkam refers to those verses of the Holy Quran in which the rulings and laws of Islamic Shariah have been stated.

These verses cover topics such as:

  • Worship (Ibadat) — Salah, Fasting, Zakat, Hajj
  • Transactions (Mu'amalat) — Trade, contracts, loans
  • Ethics & Morality (Akhlaqiyat)
  • Economics (Ma'ashiyat)
  • Social Conduct (Mu'asharat)
  • Criminal Law (Hudood & Qisas) — Punishments and retribution

Islamic jurists (Fuqaha) use these verses as the foundation for the principles of Islamic law.

The Quran is not merely a book of spiritual guidance — it is a complete constitution of life (Dastoor-e-Hayat). Through it, Allah elevates nations to greatness or brings them to decline. History bears witness to this truth.
