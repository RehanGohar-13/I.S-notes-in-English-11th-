After studying this lesson, students will be able to:

  1. Understand the importance of the compilation of Hadith in the context of the authority and preservation of Hadith.
  2. Learn about the collection and compilation of the Prophet's ﷺ Hadith during the second and third eras.
  3. Gain awareness of the books of Hadith (Sihah-e-Sittah and Kutub-e-Arba'a) and the types of Hadith.

📖 Introduction

The Holy Quran is the foundation and basis of the religion (Deen) and Shariah. It is the divine book that Allah revealed to His final Prophet, Muhammad ﷺ, and Allah entrusted the Prophet ﷺ with the responsibility of its explanation, interpretation, and clarification.

📌 What is Hadith?

Hadith refers to the saying, action, tacit approval, or attribute of the Holy Prophet ﷺ.

The Four Components of Hadith:

1. Qawl (قول) — Saying

Any statement or words spoken by the Prophet ﷺ.

2. Fi'l (فعل) — Action

Any deed or action performed by the Prophet ﷺ.

3. Taqreer (تقریر) — Tacit Approval

A statement or action done in the presence of the Prophet ﷺ which he ﷺ did not forbid.

4. Sifaat (صفات) — Attributes

Physical and personal attributes (Shama'il) of the Prophet ﷺ.

⭐ The Importance of Following the Hadith

Just as believing in the Quran is a fundamental duty, following the blessed Ahadith is also a basic requirement of the religion. The sayings and actions of the Prophet ﷺ are the foundation of:

  • Knowledge of Allah (Ma'rifat-e-Ilahi)
  • Understanding the verses of the Holy Quran
  • All fundamental beliefs including Paradise and Hell
  • All fundamental religious actions
The Muslim Ummah is unanimously agreed that the Quran cannot be understood nor acted upon without the sayings and actions of the Prophet ﷺ.

🌟 The Prophet ﷺ as Uswa-e-Hasana

Allah has declared the Prophet ﷺ as Uswa-e-Hasana — the best example — and made following him obligatory upon all Muslims.

وَمَا اٰتٰكُمُ الرَّسُوْلُ فَخُذُوْهُ وَ مَا نَهٰكُمْ عَنْهُ فَانْتَهُوْا "Whatever the Messenger ﷺ gives you — take it; and whatever he forbids you from — refrain from it." Surah Al-Hashr 59:7

📚 Tadween-e-Hadith — Compilation of Hadith

The preservation of Ahadith was arranged just as systematically as the Quran. The Companions (RA) memorized, wrote, compiled, and transmitted them to future generations.

🕐 First Period — Era of the Prophet ﷺ, Companions & Successors

Written collections of Ahadith existed with Companions including:

  • Hazrat Ali (RA), Hazrat Abu Hurairah (RA), Hazrat Jabir bin Abdullah (RA), Hazrat Abdullah bin Amr (RA)

During the era of the Tabi'een (Successors), compilation became a full movement. Key figures: Hazrat Sa'eed bin Musayyib, Hazrat Hasan Basri, and Muhammad bin Sireen (RA). Alongside collection, systematic verification of Ahadith also began.

🕑 Second Period — Era of Hazrat Umar bin Abdul Aziz (99 AH)

Formal official compilation began under Hazrat Umar bin Abdul Aziz (RA).

Key scholars: Imam Sha'bi, Imam Ja'far Sadiq, Imam Abu Hanifa, Imam Malik, Imam Sufyan Thawri, Imam Ibn Shihab Zuhri, Imam Abdur Razzaq, Imam Shafi'i (RA).

Ahadith were divided into chapters and compiled as: Muwatta, Musnad, Sunan, Musannaf, and Jami'.

Examples: Muwatta Imam Malik, Musnad Ahmad bin Hanbal, Musannaf Abdur Razzaq, Musannaf Ibn Abi Shaybah.

🕒 Third Period — 3rd Century AH (The Golden Age)

Compilation reached its peak. The claim of Deniers of Hadith and Orientalists that this was merely the era of collection is incorrect — it was the era of promotion and dissemination.

  • Collected Ahadith from across the world
  • Verified authenticity (Sihhah) and weakness (Da'f)
  • Scrutinized chains of narration (Isnad)
  • Developed Ilm Asma'ur-Rijaal and Jarh wa Ta'deel
  • Separated Sahih from Da'if Ahadith

🌟 Selected Ahadith (منتخب احادیثِ مبارکہ)

All 9 prescribed Ahadith for this chapter. Understand, memorize, and reflect on each.

Hadith 1 — On Seeking Knowledge of the Deen

مَنْ يُّرِدِ اللّٰهُ بِهٖ خَيْرًا يُّفَقِّهْهُ فِي الدِّيْنِ "Whomever Allah wishes good for, He grants him understanding of the religion."

Sahih Bukhari: 71 Al-Kafi, Vol.1, p.33

Reflection: Understanding the Deen is a sign of Allah's favor. We should actively seek Islamic knowledge as it is one of the greatest blessings.

Hadith 2 — On Good Character

فَمَا شَيْءٌ اَثْقَلُ فِيْ مِيْزَانِ الْمُؤْمِنِ يَوْمَ الْقِيٰمَةِ مِنْ خُلُقٍ حَسَنٍ وَّ اِنَّ اللّٰهَ لَيُبْغِضُ الْفَاحِشَ الْبَذِيَّءَ "Nothing will be heavier in the believer's scale on the Day of Judgment than good character. And Allah dislikes the shameless and foul-mouthed person."

Jami' Tirmidhi: 2002 Mustadrak al-Wasa'il, Vol.8, p.443

Reflection: Good character outweighs even many acts of worship on the Day of Judgment. Allah dislikes indecency and foul language.

Hadith 3 — On Helping Fellow Muslims

مَنْ نَفَّسَ عَنْ مُؤْمِنٍ كُرْبَةً مِّنْ كُرَبِ الدُّنْيَا نَفَّسَ اللّٰهُ عَنْهُ كُرْبَةً مِّنْ كُرَبِ يَوْمِ الْقِيَامَةِ وَمَنْ يَّسَّرَ عَلَىٰ مُعْسِرٍ يَّسَّرَ اللّٰهُ عَلَيْهِ فِي الدُّنْيَا وَالْآخِرَةِ وَمَنْ سَتَرَ مُسْلِمًا سَتَرَه اللّٰهُ فِي الدُّنْيَا وَالْآخِرَةِ وَاللّٰهُ فِي عَوْنِ الْعَبْدِ مَا كَانَ الْعَبْدُ فِي عَوْنِ أَخِيْهِ "Whoever relieves a believer of a worldly hardship, Allah will relieve him of a hardship on the Day of Judgment. Whoever makes things easy for one in difficulty, Allah will make things easy for him in this world and the Hereafter. Whoever covers (the faults of) a Muslim, Allah will cover his faults in this world and the Hereafter. And Allah continues to help His servant as long as the servant helps his brother."

Sahih Muslim: 6853, 2699 Wasa'il al-Shia, Vol.16, p.372

Reflection: This Hadith establishes a beautiful divine principle: what you do for others, Allah does for you. Relieve someone's pain → Allah relieves yours. Help someone in difficulty → Allah helps you. Cover someone's faults → Allah covers yours. And the promise is continuous — Allah's help never stops as long as you keep helping your fellow human beings.

Hadith 4 — On Accountability on the Day of Judgment

لَا تَزُوْلُ قَدَمَا عَبْدٍ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ حَتَّىٰ يُسْأَلَ عَنْ عُمْرِہٖ فِيْمَا أَفْنَاہُ وَعَنْ عِلْمِہٖ فِيْمَا فَعَلَ وَعَنْ مَالِہٖ مِنْ أَيْنَ اكْتَسَبَہٗ وَفِيْمَا أَنْفَقَہٗ وَعَنْ جِسْمِہٖ فِيْمَا أَبْلَاہُ "The feet of a servant will not move on the Day of Judgment until he is questioned about four things: his life — how he spent it; his knowledge — how he acted upon it; his wealth — how he earned it and where he spent it; and his body — how he used it."

Jami' Tirmidhi: 2416 Bihar al-Anwar, Vol.36, p.80

Reflection: Every single resource Allah gave us will be audited:
  • Life (Umr) — Did you waste your time or use it productively?
  • Knowledge (Ilm) — Did you act on what you learned?
  • Wealth (Maal) — Was it earned lawfully (Halal) and spent wisely?
  • Body (Jism) — Did you use your health in obedience to Allah?
This Hadith should make us deeply conscious of how we use every blessing.

Hadith 5 — The Seven Destructive Sins

اِجْتَنِبُوا السَّبْعَ الْمُوْبِقَاتِ قَالُوْا: يَا رَسُوْلَ اللّٰهِ وَمَا هُنَّ؟ قَالَ: اَلشِّرْكُ بِاللّٰهِ، وَالسِّحْرُ، وَقَتْلُ النَّفْسِ الَّتِي حَرَّمَ اللّٰهُ إِلَّا بِالْحَقِّ، وَأَكْلُ الرِّبَا، وَأَكْلُ مَالِ الْيَتِيْمِ، وَالتَّوَلِّي يَوْمَ الزَّحْفِ، وَقَذْفُ الْمُحْصَنَاتِ الْمُؤْمِنَاتِ الْغَافِلَاتِ "Avoid the seven destructive sins." The Companions asked: "O Messenger of Allah, what are they?" He ﷺ said: "Associating partners with Allah (Shirk), practicing magic (Sihr), killing a soul that Allah has made sacred except by right, consuming interest (Riba), consuming the wealth of an orphan, fleeing from the battlefield, and slandering chaste, innocent, believing women."

Sahih Bukhari: 2766 Wasa'il al-Shia, Vol.15, p.331

The Seven Destructive Sins (Al-Moobiqat):
  1. Shirk — Associating partners with Allah (the gravest sin)
  2. Sihr — Practicing magic/sorcery
  3. Unlawful killing — Taking a life that Allah has sanctified
  4. Riba — Consuming interest/usury
  5. Orphan's wealth — Misappropriating an orphan's property
  6. Fleeing battle — Running away from the battlefield
  7. Slander — Falsely accusing chaste, believing women
These are called "destructive" (Moobiqat) because they destroy a person's faith, character, and ultimately their Hereafter.

Hadith 6 — On Enjoining Good and Forbidding Evil

مَنْ رَأَىٰ مِنْكُمْ مُنْكَرًا فَلْيُغَيِّرْهُ بِيَدِهِ فَإِنْ لَّمْ يَسْتَطِعْ فَبِلِسَانِهِ فَإِنْ لَّمْ يَسْتَطِعْ فَبِقَلْبِهِ وَذٰلِكَ أَضْعَفُ الْإِيْمَانِ "Whoever among you sees an evil, let him change it with his hand (power). If he is unable, then with his tongue (speech). If he is unable, then with his heart (consider it wrong) — and that is the weakest level of faith."

Sahih Muslim: 177 Mustadrak al-Wasa'il, Vol.12, p.193

Three Levels of Stopping Evil:
  • 🤚 By hand (action) — If you have the authority and power, physically stop the wrong.
  • 🗣️ By tongue (speech) — If you can't act, speak out against it.
  • ❤️ By heart (feeling) — If you can't even speak, at least hate the evil in your heart. This is the minimum level of faith.
A Muslim can never be indifferent to evil. Even the weakest faith demands that you recognize wrong as wrong.

Hadith 7 — On Two Undervalued Blessings

نِعْمَتَانِ مَغْبُوْنٌ فِيْهِمَا كَثِيْرٌ مِّنَ النَّاسِ: اَلصِّحَّةُ وَالْفَرَاغُ "There are two blessings in which many people are at a loss: health and free time."

Sahih Bukhari: 6412 Safinat al-Bihar, Vol.6, p.601

Reflection: The word "Maghboon" means someone who is cheated or at a loss in a trade. The Prophet ﷺ is telling us that most people "lose out" on two priceless blessings:
  • Health (Sehhat) — We only value it when we fall sick.
  • Free time (Faraghat) — We only miss it when we become busy.
Use your health and youth to worship Allah, seek knowledge, and serve others — before they are taken away.

Hadith 8 — On Halal, Haram, and Doubtful Matters

اَلْحَلَالُ بَيِّنٌ وَالْحَرَامُ بَيِّنٌ وَبَيْنَهُمَا مُشْتَبِهَاتٌ لَا يَعْلَمُهُنَّ كَثِيْرٌ مِّنَ النَّاسِ فَمَنِ اتَّقَى الشُّبُهَاتِ اسْتَبْرَأَ لِدِيْنِهِ وَعِرْضِهِ وَمَنْ وَقَعَ فِي الشُّبُهَاتِ وَقَعَ فِي الْحَرَامِ كَالرَّاعِي حَوْلَ الْحِمَى يُوْشِكُ أَنْ يُّوَاقِعَهُ أَلَا وَإِنَّ لِكُلِّ مَلِكٍ حِمًى أَلَا وَإِنَّ حِمَى اللّٰهِ فِي أَرْضِهِ مَحَارِمُهُ "The Halal (lawful) is clear and the Haram (unlawful) is clear, and between them are doubtful matters that many people do not know about. So whoever avoids the doubtful matters has safeguarded his religion and his honor. And whoever falls into the doubtful matters falls into the Haram — like a shepherd who grazes his animals around a protected pasture; he is about to enter it. Indeed, every king has a protected zone, and indeed, Allah's protected zone on His earth is His prohibitions."

Sahih Bukhari: 52 Al-Hidaya li-Shi'at A'immah al-Huda, Vol.1, p.613

Reflection: This is one of the most comprehensive Ahadith in Islam. It teaches us:
  • Halal is clear — no confusion about what is permitted.
  • Haram is clear — no confusion about what is forbidden.
  • The grey area (Mushtabihat) is where most people slip up.
  • The shepherd analogy is brilliant: if you keep grazing near the forbidden zone, eventually your animals will wander in. Similarly, if you keep engaging in doubtful things, you will eventually fall into clear Haram.
  • Precaution (Taqwa) means staying far from the boundary, not testing how close you can get.

Hadith 9 — On Prohibition of Imitating the Opposite Gender

لَعَنَ رَسُوْلُ اللّٰهِ ﷺ الْمُتَشَبِّهِينَ مِنَ الرِّجَالِ بِالنِّسَاءِ وَالْمُتَشَبِّهَاتِ مِنَ النِّسَاءِ بِالرِّجَالِ "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ cursed those men who imitate women, and those women who imitate men."

Sahih Bukhari: 5885 Bihar al-Anwar, Vol.76, p.66

Reflection: Islam assigns distinct identities and roles to men and women, and each gender is honored in its own right. The Prophet ﷺ strongly condemned the practice of men adopting the appearance, dress, mannerisms, or behavior of women, and vice versa. This teaching preserves the natural order (Fitrah) that Allah created and protects the dignity and identity of both genders.

Hadith 10 — On Cursing Parents (Indirectly)

إِنَّ مِنْ أَكْبَرِ الْكَبَائِرِ أَنْ يَّلْعَنَ الرَّجُلُ وَالِدَيْهِ قِيلَ: يَا رَسُولَ اللّٰهِ وَكَيْفَ يَلْعَنُ الرَّجُلُ وَالِدَيْهِ؟ قَالَ: يَسُبُّ الرَّجُلُ أَبَا الرَّجُلِ فَيَسُبُّ أَبَاهُ وَيَسُبُّ أُمَّهُ "Among the greatest of major sins is that a man curses his own parents." It was asked: "O Messenger of Allah ﷺ, how can a man curse his own parents?" He ﷺ said: "He insults another man's father, so that man insults his father in return, and insults his mother."

Sahih Bukhari: 5973 Bihar al-Anwar, Vol.71, p.47

Reflection: This Hadith reveals a profound social wisdom. You might never directly curse your own parents, but if you insult someone else's parents, they will retaliate by insulting yours — and thus you become the cause of your own parents being cursed. Islam teaches us to respect ALL people's parents, because disrespect circulates. This is a powerful lesson in social responsibility and the law of cause and effect.

Hadith 11 — On the Prayer of the Oppressed

اتَّقِ دَعْوَةَ الْمَظْلُومِ فَإِنَّهَا لَيْسَ بَيْنَهَا وَبَيْنَ اللّٰهِ حِجَابٌ "Beware of the prayer (supplication) of the oppressed, for there is no barrier between it and Allah."

Sahih Bukhari: 2448 Nahj al-Fasahah, p.162

Reflection: This is one of the most powerful warnings in Islam. When someone is wronged and they raise their hands to Allah in desperation, that prayer goes straight to Allah with no filter, no delay, and no barrier. It doesn't matter if the oppressed person is a believer or not, rich or poor, powerful or weak. This Hadith should terrify anyone who oppresses others and comfort anyone who is being oppressed.

Hadith 12 — On the Authority of the Prophet's ﷺ Sunnah

يُوشِكُ الرَّجُلُ مُتَّكِئًا عَلَىٰ أَرِيكَتِهِ يُحَدَّثُ بِحَدِيثِي مِنْ حَدِيثِي فَيَقُولُ بَيْنَنَا وَبَيْنَكُمْ كِتَابُ اللّٰهِ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ مَا وَجَدْنَا فِيهِ مِنْ حَلَالٍ اسْتَحْلَلْنَاهُ وَمَا وَجَدْنَا فِيهِ مِنْ حَرَامٍ حَرَّمْنَاهُ أَلَا وَإِنَّ مَا حَرَّمَ رَسُولُ اللّٰهِ ﷺ مِثْلُ مَا حَرَّمَ اللّٰهُ "Soon there will come a time when a man, reclining on his couch, will be told a Hadith of mine and he will say: 'Between us and you is the Book of Allah. Whatever we find in it as Halal, we will consider Halal, and whatever we find as Haram, we will consider Haram.' (The Prophet ﷺ then said:) Beware! Whatever the Messenger of Allah ﷺ has declared Haram is just as Haram as what Allah has declared Haram."

Sunan Ibn Majah: 12 Riyad al-Salikin, Vol.3, p.381

Reflection: This Hadith is a direct prophecy and a direct refutation of the "Quran-only" mindset (Munkireen-e-Hadith). The Prophet ﷺ predicted that people would come who would reject his Sunnah and claim that the Quran alone is sufficient. He ﷺ made it crystal clear: what he declared Haram carries the same weight as what Allah declared Haram, because his commands come from Allah. This is the strongest proof that the Hadith is an inseparable part of Islamic law.

Hadith 13 — On Leaving Doubtful Things

دَعْ مَا يَرِيبُكَ إِلَىٰ مَا لَا يَرِيبُكَ فَإِنَّ الصِّدْقَ طُمَأْنِينَةٌ وَإِنَّ الْكَذِبَ رِيبَةٌ "Leave that which puts you in doubt and adopt that which does not put you in doubt. Indeed, truthfulness brings tranquility, and indeed, falsehood brings doubt."

Jami' Tirmidhi: 2518 Wasa'il al-Shia, Vol.27, p.168

Reflection: This Hadith gives us a simple but powerful decision-making principle: when in doubt, choose the path that gives your heart peace. Truth and honesty bring inner calm (Tum'aninah), while lies and deception create restlessness and anxiety (Reebah). Your conscience is your internal compass — if something feels wrong, leave it.

Hadith 14 — On Love for the Prophet ﷺ

لَا يُؤْمِنُ أَحَدُكُمْ حَتَّىٰ أَكُونَ أَحَبَّ إِلَيْهِ مِنْ وَالِدِهِ وَوَلَدِهِ وَالنَّاسِ أَجْمَعِينَ "None of you can be a (true) believer until I become more beloved to him than his father, his children, and all of mankind."

Sahih Bukhari & Muslim: 15 Bihar al-Anwar, Vol.17, p.14

Reflection: This Hadith sets the standard of true faith (Iman). Love for the Prophet ﷺ is not optional or sentimental — it is a condition of faith itself. This love must surpass even the deepest natural bonds: the love for one's parents and children. This is because the Prophet ﷺ is the one who guided us to Allah, and without his guidance, we would be lost. True love for the Prophet ﷺ means following his Sunnah in every aspect of life.

Hadith 15 — On the Most Beloved Deeds to Allah

عَنْ عَبْدِ اللّٰهِ قَالَ سَأَلْتُ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ أَيُّ الْعَمَلِ أَحَبُّ إِلَى اللّٰهِ قَالَ: اَلصَّلَاةُ عَلَىٰ وَقْتِهَا قَالَ: ثُمَّ أَيُّ قَالَ: ثُمَّ بِرُّ الْوَالِدَيْنِ قَالَ: ثُمَّ أَيُّ قَالَ: اَلْجِهَادُ فِي سَبِيلِ اللّٰهِ "Hazrat Abdullah (RA) reported: I asked the Prophet ﷺ, 'Which deed is most beloved to Allah?' He ﷺ said: 'Prayer (Salah) at its appointed time.' I asked: 'Then which?' He ﷺ said: 'Being good and dutiful to one's parents.' I asked: 'Then which?' He ﷺ said: 'Jihad (striving) in the way of Allah.'"

Sahih Bukhari & Muslim: 527 Mustadrak al-Wasa'il, Vol.3, p.43

The Top 3 Most Beloved Deeds to Allah:
  1. 🤲 Salah on time — The most beloved act. Not just praying, but praying at its proper time. This shows discipline and priority.
  2. 👨‍👩‍👧 Being good to parents (Birr-ul-Walidayn) — Ranked second, even above Jihad! This shows the immense status of parents in Islam.
  3. ⚔️ Jihad in the way of Allah — Striving and struggling for Allah's cause.
Notice the order: worship → family → society. Islam builds a righteous individual first, then a righteous family, then a righteous community.
