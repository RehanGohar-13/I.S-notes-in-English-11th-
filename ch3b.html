Chapter 3(B): The Prophet ﷺ as an Ideal Head of State

The foundation of Madinah, governance, diplomacy & the servant-leadership model

Foundation of the State of Madinah

Like every other sphere of life, the blessed life of the Prophet ﷺ as a head of government is an ideal model. He ﷺ laid the foundation of the State of Madinah and then devised a strategy for its strength, expansion, internal and external peace, and the welfare of its citizens — a beacon of guidance for all rulers.

🏛️ Core Principle

"The leader of a nation is its servant."

📜 Meezaq-e-Madinah

The Charter of Madinah — the first written constitution — established a peace treaty with the Jewish tribes of Madinah, making the city a cradle of peace and security.

🤝 Muwakhat-e-Madinah

The Brotherhood of Madinah — united the Muhajireen (emigrants) and Ansar (helpers) into a single bond of brotherhood, strengthening internal solidarity.

🕌 Masjid-e-Nabawi

Given central importance for conducting state affairs — it served as the seat of government, court, educational centre and community hub.

The Prophet ﷺ managed the State of Madinah with extraordinary wisdom and foresight:

  • Internal affairs: Special attention to peace & security, stability, development, and moral training.
  • External affairs: Staying informed about enemies of Islam, strengthening the defence of Madinah, inviting other states to Islam, and improving diplomatic relations.
• • •

Key Elements of State Policy

1. Spread of Islam (Ashat-e-Islam)

The highest priority in the Prophet's ﷺ state policy was the spread of Islam, propagation of the Deen, and Amr bil Maroof wa Nahy anil Munkar (enjoining good and forbidding evil).

  • A structured system of religious education and propagation existed in the Muslim state — the fundamental duty of an Islamic government.
  • Sa'eed bin Aas (RA) was appointed for teaching duties.
  • Suffah was a regular educational institution with a permanent arrangement for teaching and training. From here, the Prophet ﷺ would dispatch Sahaba (RA) to various tribes for the spread of Islam and religious education.

2. Respect for Humanity (Ihtiram-e-Insaniyat)

An important aspect of the ideal state established in Madinah was the respect for humanity. The Prophet ﷺ forbade violence for the establishment of social peace and taught mutual respect.

"مخلوق اللہ تعالیٰ کا کنبہ ہے، اللہ تعالیٰ کے نزدیک سب سے زیادہ محبوب شخص وہ ہے جو اللہ تعالیٰ کے کنبے کے ساتھ اچھا سلوک کرتا ہے۔"
"All of creation is the family of Allah. The most beloved person to Allah is the one who treats His family well."
— Shu'ab-ul-Iman: 2528

🛡️ Protection of Non-Muslim Citizens

The Prophet ﷺ said:

"Whoever oppresses a non-Muslim living in the Islamic state, or usurps his right, or burdens him beyond his capacity, or takes anything from him without his willing consent — I will be the advocate of that non-Muslim against the Muslim on the Day of Judgment."
— Sunan Abu Dawood: 3052

3. Administration of the State of Madinah

To protect the Islamic state from chaos and disorder and to ensure its stability, the Prophet ﷺ took continuous measures:

  • Internal stability: Through Muwakhat-e-Madinah and Meezaq-e-Madinah, Madinah was strengthened internally and externally.
  • Migration policy: Any person, family or tribe in Arabia that accepted Islam was encouraged to migrate to Madinah or its suburbs — increasing the population strengthened the military and political position and facilitated the education and training of new Muslims.
  • Departments: Various departments were established to handle state affairs.
  • Consultation (Mashwarah): The Prophet ﷺ consulted the Sahaba (RA) and the Ummahat-ul-Mu'mineen (RA) in various matters.
  • Governors: Governors were appointed in different regions for administrative duties and the promotion of the Deen. The famous example is Mu'adh bin Jabal (RA) being sent as governor to Yemen.

💰 Salary System

The Prophet ﷺ introduced a salary system for government workers, but they received only enough to meet their needs — no excess. He ﷺ taught that any position, office, responsibility or authority is more of a responsibility than an honour.

"تم میں سے ہر ایک ذمہ دار ہے۔ ہر ایک سے اس کی ذمہ داریوں کے بارے میں سوال کیا جائے گا۔"
"Every one of you is a guardian, and every one of you will be questioned about his responsibilities."
— Sahih Bukhari: 893
• • •

Defence & Intelligence

After the Hijrah, hostile forces surrounded the State of Madinah on all sides. To stay informed about the enemy, the Prophet ﷺ established an intelligence system through which he remained aware of the enemies' conditions, intentions and plans.

Key defence measures included:

  • Blockade of the Syrian trade route — weakened the economic power of the Quraish.
  • Diplomatic alliances with allied tribes — increasing the number of Muslim allies and strengthening their position.
• • •

Foreign Policy

🕊️ Sulh-e-Hudaibiyah

The Treaty of Hudaibiyah is called the masterpiece of the Prophet's ﷺ foreign policy. It demonstrated extraordinary diplomatic foresight and strategic patience.

📨 Letters to World Leaders

The most important principle of the Prophet's ﷺ foreign policy was the promotion of the invitation to Islam. He ﷺ wrote letters of dawah to various kings and rulers of the world, spreading his message to the leaders of many lands.

  • Those kings who accepted the invitation — Allah honoured them.
  • Those who rejected it — their power did not last long.
  • Eventually, all those regions became part of the Muslim state.

Question 2: Short Answers

(i) What measures did the Prophet ﷺ take to stay informed about the enemies? ▼

After the Hijrah, hostile forces surrounded Madinah on all sides. The Prophet ﷺ established a comprehensive intelligence system (siraag rasaani) through which he remained constantly aware of the enemies' conditions, intentions and plans. Additionally, he blockaded the Syrian trade route to weaken the Quraish economically, and formed diplomatic alliances with friendly tribes to strengthen the Muslim position and isolate the enemies.

(ii) What benefits did the Islamic state gain from treaties with surrounding tribes? ▼

The treaties with surrounding tribes provided the following benefits:

  • Increased allies: The number of Muslim friends and allies grew, strengthening the state's position.
  • External stability: The threat from hostile forces was reduced as neutral tribes became allies.
  • Military strength: Allied tribes could provide support during conflicts.
  • Economic security: Trade routes became safer for Muslim merchants.
  • Spread of Islam: Peaceful relations opened doors for dawah among these tribes.
(iii) What guidance does the educational system of the State of Madinah provide for us? ▼

The educational system of Madinah provides the following guidance:

  • Education is the state's primary duty: The Prophet ﷺ made religious education and propagation the highest priority of governance.
  • Institutional learning: Suffah served as a regular, permanent educational institution — showing the need for structured schools.
  • Appointed teachers: Sa'eed bin Aas (RA) was specifically appointed for teaching — highlighting the importance of qualified educators.
  • Field deployment: Sahaba (RA) were dispatched from Suffah to various tribes for dawah and teaching — a model of outreach education.
  • Moral training alongside academics: Education was not merely intellectual but included tarbiyah (character building).
(iv) How much salary did government employees receive from Bait-ul-Maal in the Prophetic era? ▼

The Prophet ﷺ introduced a salary system for government workers, but they received only enough to meet their basic needs — no more. The principle was that public office is a responsibility, not a privilege. The Prophet ﷺ taught: "Every one of you is a guardian, and every one of you will be questioned about his responsibilities" (Bukhari: 893). This prevented corruption, extravagance and the misuse of public funds.

(v) What were the effects of the Prophet's ﷺ foreign policy? ▼

The effects of the Prophet's ﷺ foreign policy were far-reaching:

  • Sulh-e-Hudaibiyah — the masterpiece of his diplomacy — brought a period of peace that allowed Islam to spread rapidly.
  • Letters to world rulers (Byzantine Emperor, Persian King, Negus of Abyssinia, etc.) carried the message of Islam to the highest levels of global leadership.
  • Kings who accepted the invitation were honoured by Allah; those who rejected it saw their empires crumble.
  • Eventually, all those regions became part of the Muslim state.
  • Diplomatic alliances with tribes isolated the enemies and strengthened the Muslim position.

Question 3: Detailed Answer

Shed light on the achievements of the Prophet ﷺ as a head of state. ▼

Introduction

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was not only a spiritual guide but also the founder and head of the first Islamic state in Madinah. His governance model — built on the principle that "the leader of a nation is its servant" — remains an unmatched example for all rulers.

1. Foundation of the State

The Prophet ﷺ laid the foundations of the State of Madinah through three landmark steps:

  • Masjid-e-Nabawi: Built as the central hub for state affairs, worship, education and justice.
  • Muwakhat-e-Madinah: The Brotherhood pact that united the Muhajireen and Ansar, settling the emigrants and creating internal solidarity.
  • Meezaq-e-Madinah: The first written constitution — a peace treaty with the Jewish tribes that made Madinah a cradle of security.

2. Spread of Islam & Education

The highest priority of state policy was the propagation of Islam and Amr bil Maroof wa Nahy anil Munkar. Suffah was established as a permanent educational institution, Sa'eed bin Aas (RA) was appointed as a teacher, and Sahaba were dispatched to various tribes for dawah and tarbiyah.

3. Respect for Humanity

The Prophet ﷺ declared: "All of creation is the family of Allah" (Shu'ab-ul-Iman: 2528). He guaranteed the protection of non-Muslim citizens, declaring that he would personally advocate for any oppressed non-Muslim on the Day of Judgment (Abu Dawood: 3052). Violence was forbidden and mutual respect was enforced.

4. Administration & Governance

The Prophet ﷺ established various administrative departments, appointed governors (e.g. Mu'adh bin Jabal (RA) to Yemen), introduced a modest salary system for government workers, and practised consultation (Mashwarah) with the Sahaba and Ummahat-ul-Mu'mineen. He taught: "Every one of you is a guardian, and every one of you will be questioned about his responsibilities" (Bukhari: 893).

5. Defence & Intelligence

A comprehensive intelligence network was established to monitor enemy movements. The Syrian trade route was blockaded to weaken the Quraish economically. Diplomatic alliances with friendly tribes strengthened the Muslim military position.

6. Foreign Policy & Diplomacy

Sulh-e-Hudaibiyah stands as the masterpiece of the Prophet's ﷺ foreign policy — a strategic treaty that paved the way for the peaceful spread of Islam. He wrote letters of dawah to the kings of the world (Byzantine, Persian, Abyssinian, Egyptian, etc.). Those who accepted were honoured; those who rejected lost their empires. Eventually, all those regions became part of the Muslim state.

Conclusion

The Prophet ﷺ as a head of state combined justice with mercy, strength with diplomacy, and authority with humility. His model of servant-leadership, institutional governance, respect for all citizens, and visionary foreign policy remains the gold standard for governance in any era.
