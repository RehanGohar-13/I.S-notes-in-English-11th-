Q2. Short Questions

1. The Holy Quran is a Universal Book — explain briefly. ▼

The Quran addresses all of humanity, not a specific nation or race. Previous books were for particular communities, but the Quran uses "O Mankind!" to speak to everyone. Allah says: "It is nothing but a reminder for all the worlds." (81:27). Its teachings apply to every era, region, and race.

2. Write briefly on the Eternity (Abdiyyat) of the Holy Quran. ▼

The Quran's teachings are everlasting. They are so natural (fitri) that people of every age feel they were revealed specifically for them. The Quran remains equally beneficial and practicable for all people until the Day of Judgment.

5. Write briefly on Ayat-ul-Ahkam (Verses of Rulings). ▼

Ayat-ul-Ahkam are Quranic verses containing Shariah rulings and laws covering worship, transactions, ethics, economics, social conduct, and criminal law (Hudood & Qisas). Islamic jurists use these as the foundation of Islamic law.

Q3. Detailed Question

Write a detailed note on the characteristics of the Holy Quran. ▼

The Holy Quran possesses four major characteristics:

1. Universality: Addresses all humanity, not a specific nation. Uses "O Mankind!" (14:52).

2. Completeness: The chain of revelation from Adam (AS) was perfected on the Prophet ﷺ. It is a complete code of life.

3. Comprehensiveness: Covers beliefs, worship, transactions, social conduct, morality, and laws. "A clear explanation of everything." (16:89)

4. Eternity: Teachings are forever, natural (fitri), and practicable in every era until the Day of Judgment.

Together, these make the Quran the final, universal, and eternal constitution of human life.

Q2. Short Questions (Hadith)

1. Briefly describe the importance of Hadith. ▼

The Hadith is the second primary source of Islamic guidance after the Quran. The sayings and actions of the Prophet ﷺ are the foundation of our knowledge of Allah, our understanding of Quranic verses, and all our fundamental beliefs and actions. The Muslim Ummah is unanimously agreed that the Quran cannot be understood or acted upon without the Hadith. Allah Himself commanded: "Whatever the Messenger gives you, take it." (59:7)

2. Name any four Companions (RA) who had collections of Ahadith. ▼
  1. Hazrat Ali (RA)
  2. Hazrat Abu Hurairah (RA)
  3. Hazrat Jabir bin Abdullah (RA)
  4. Hazrat Abdullah bin Amr (RA)
3. How did the Companions (RA) arrange for the preservation of Hadith? ▼

The Companions (RA) preserved the Ahadith through multiple methods:

  • Memorization (Hifz) — They memorized the Prophet's ﷺ sayings and actions.
  • Writing (Kitabat) — They wrote down Ahadith in personal collections.
  • Compilation (Tadween) — They formally arranged and organized the Ahadith.
  • Practice (Amal) — They transmitted the Ahadith to the next generation through their own actions and teachings.
4. Name any two important personalities of the second period of Hadith compilation. ▼

Two prominent scholars of the second period (era of Umar bin Abdul Aziz, 99 AH):

  1. Imam Malik (RA) — Compiler of Muwatta
  2. Imam Ibn Shihab Zuhri (RA) — One of the first scholars formally commissioned to compile Ahadith

Other notable figures include Imam Abu Hanifa, Imam Shafi'i, Imam Sufyan Thawri, and Imam Abdur Razzaq (RA).

Q3. Detailed Question (Hadith)

Write a detailed note on the compilation of Hadith (Tadween-e-Hadith) and its periods. ▼

The compilation of Hadith (Tadween-e-Hadith) took place in three main periods:

First Period — Era of the Prophet ﷺ, Companions & Successors

During the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime and the era of the Companions (RA), Ahadith were memorized, written, and preserved in personal collections. Companions like Hazrat Ali, Abu Hurairah, Jabir bin Abdullah, and Abdullah bin Amr (RA) maintained written collections. During the era of the Tabi'een (Successors), this became a formal movement led by scholars like Sa'eed bin Musayyib, Hasan Basri, and Muhammad bin Sireen (RA). Verification of Ahadith also began.

Second Period — Era of Umar bin Abdul Aziz (99 AH)

Formal, official compilation began under the Caliph Umar bin Abdul Aziz (RA). Great scholars like Imam Malik, Imam Abu Hanifa, Imam Shafi'i, Imam Ibn Shihab Zuhri, and Imam Abdur Razzaq (RA) contributed. Ahadith were organized into chapters and compiled as Muwatta, Musnad, Sunan, and Musannaf. Alongside Prophetic Ahadith, sayings of the Companions and Tabi'een were also compiled.

Third Period — 3rd Century AH (Golden Age)

Compilation reached its peak. Muhadditheen collected Ahadith from across the world, verified their authenticity, scrutinized chains of narration (Isnad), and developed the sciences of Ilm Asma'ur-Rijaal and Jarh wa Ta'deel. Sahih Ahadith were separated from Da'if ones. The most authoritative books — the Sihah-e-Sittah (Sahih Bukhari, Sahih Muslim, Sunan Abi Dawud, Jami' Tirmidhi, Sunan Nasa'i, Sunan Ibn Majah) — were compiled during this era.

The claim of Orientalists and Deniers of Hadith that the third century was merely the era of collection is incorrect. Collection began in the Prophet's ﷺ own lifetime; the third century was the era of refinement, verification, and dissemination.
