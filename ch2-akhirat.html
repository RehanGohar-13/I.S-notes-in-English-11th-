Akhirat — The Hereafter

Chapter 2(E) — Faith (Imaniyat) · Belief in the Hereafter · Textbook pp. 33–37

بِسْمِ اللهِ الرَّحْمٰنِ الرَّحِيْمِ "In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful."

🎯 Learning Outcomes

After studying this lesson, students will be able to:

  1. Gain a comprehensive introduction to the belief in Akhirat, the concept of Akhirat in various religions, and its rational and scriptural proofs.
  2. Understand the concepts of Barzakh, Hashr, Mizan, Pul Siraat, Shafa'at, and Hawd-e-Kawthar.
  3. Compare the life of Jannah and Jahannam.
  4. Evaluate the importance and effects of the belief in Akhirat in practical life.
  5. Firmly believe in the temporary nature of the world and make the concern for Akhirat the primary goal of life.
  6. In light of the belief in Akhirat, become doers of maximum good deeds and avoiders of evil, striving to attain intercession.

📖 What is Akhirat?

The word Akhirat literally means "that which comes afterwards." Belief in Akhirat means that this worldly life will one day end, after which a new eternal life will begin.

After death, humans will be raised for reward, punishment, and reckoning. The day on which humans will be held accountable is called Yawm al-Akhirah (the Day of the Hereafter).

Core Idea: This world is a place of examination — Allah ﷻ gave humans free will and sent them here to be tested. This life is temporary; the life after death is eternal. The full consequences of actions are not realised in this world — they appear in the Hereafter.
· · ·

🌍 Akhirat — A Universal Belief

The belief in Akhirat is the most important belief among the beliefs of all religions. Every religion teaches that man will not perish forever after death; rather, he will be revived and recompensed for his good and evil deeds.

The deniers of Akhirat, however, reject resurrection, reckoning, and the existence of Jannah and Jahannam. In the Quran, Allah ﷻ mentions the doubts of the polytheists and answers them beautifully. The polytheists of Makkah were troubled by the question: how can a human be revived after death and decay?

Allah ﷻ responds:

وَهُوَ الَّذِي يَبْدَؤُا الْخَلْقَ ثُمَّ يُعِيدُهُ وَهُوَ أَهْوَنُ عَلَيْهِ "And He it is Who originates the creation, then will repeat it, and that is easier for Him." Surah Ar-Rum 30:27
· · ·

🧠 Rational Proof of Akhirat

The Quran establishes that reviving man after death is very easy for Allah ﷻ. Human intellect also accepts the belief in Akhirat. Every person of sound nature acknowledges that actions have consequences — good deeds earn good rewards, and evil deeds earn evil consequences.

If one were to hypothetically accept that there is no life after death, the necessary conclusion would be that man — the Ashraf-ul-Makhluqat (noblest of creation) — was created in vain, and his deeds carry no value. But it is impossible that this entire universe was created purposelessly.

"We did not create the heavens and the earth and whatever is between them in play; We created them only with truth (for a purpose)." Surah Ad-Dukhan 44:38–40
Hence sound reason demands that there must be a day when the righteous are rewarded and the wrongdoers are punished — and that day is the Day of Qiyamah.
· · ·

⏳ Stages of the Hereafter

The reward and punishment of a person's deeds begins immediately after death in the form of Barzakh. This stage continues until the blowing of the Soor (Trumpet). After Qiyamah is established, the Trumpet will be blown to gather all mankind — the stage of Barzakh ends, and the stage of Hashr begins.

Stage 1 — Barzakh (The Intermediate Life)

  • Begins immediately after death.
  • Continues until the Trumpet is blown.
  • Reward and punishment of deeds begin here in a preliminary form.

Stage 2 — Hashr (The Day of Gathering)

All humans will rise from their graves and be gathered in a vast plain to be presented before Allah ﷻ. The sun will be extremely close to creation, and people will drown in their sweat according to their deeds.

Sinners

  • Eyes lowered, humiliation covering them
  • Faces dust-ridden and darkened
  • The day will be severe and terrifying for them

Believers

  • Faces radiant with Noor
  • Under the shade of the Arsh
  • Light to their right or left
  • Receiving glad tidings of Jannah

Stage 3 — Mizan (The Weighing of Deeds)

A scale will be set up to weigh deeds. No human will be wronged even by the weight of an atom.

  • ✅ Good deeds heavier → record given in the right hand → glad tidings of Jannah.
  • ❌ Evil deeds heavier → record given in the left hand → warning of Jahannam (for disbelievers, hypocrites, and sinners).

Stage 4 — Hawd-e-Kawthar (The Pond of Kawthar)

In the plain of gathering, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ will be granted a pond of sweet water, flowing from a river of Jannah called Kawthar.

Believers will drink from the blessed hands of the Prophet ﷺ, and by its blessing they will not feel thirst until entering Jannah. Disbelievers, polytheists, and apostates will be denied this water.

Stage 5 — Pul Siraat (The Bridge)

A bridge known as Pul Siraat or Jisr-e-Jahannam stretches across Hell. Flames of Jahannam blaze beneath it; Jannah lies on the other side.

  • The righteous cross it in the blink of an eye.
  • Sinners struggle according to the weight of their sins.
  • Those with heavy sins will fall into Jahannam.

Stage 6 — Jannah or Jahannam

  • Sinful believers (fasiq/fajir) will eventually be removed from Hell after their punishment is complete.
  • Disbelievers will remain in Jahannam forever.

🌟 Shafa'at (Intercession) on the Day of Qiyamah

The word Shafa'at means "recommendation / intercession." It is of two types:

Shafa'at-e-Kubra

The Greatest Intercession

  • Exclusive to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ
  • Benefits the whole of humanity
  • Through it, the severity of waiting for reckoning ends
  • Known as Maqam-e-Mahmood

The Prophet ﷺ himself said: "Maqam-e-Mahmood is intercession." Jami' Tirmidhi: 3148

Shafa'at-e-Sughra

The Lesser Intercession

Granted by Allah's permission to:

  • Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and other Prophets (AS)
  • Angels (Malaika)
  • Scholars (Ulama) and Martyrs (Shuhada)
  • The Righteous (Saliheen)
  • The Holy Quran
  • Fasting (Roza)
Crucial Point: There will be no force or compulsion in intercession. No one has power over Allah ﷻ. He will grant permission for intercession to whomever He wills.

💫 Effects of Belief in Akhirat on Practical Life

The important effects of the belief in Akhirat on human life are as follows:

1️⃣ Inclination Towards Good Deeds

A believer is certain that all his deeds are in Allah's sight and will be rewarded on the Day of Qiyamah. Hence he strives to perform good deeds abundantly so his scale of good deeds may be heavy.

2️⃣ Avoidance of Evil

He is convinced that this world is Dar-ul-Amal (abode of action) and that every single deed will be recompensed. So he avoids evil to escape punishment.

3️⃣ Respect for Rights

He respects duties and the rights of others because if he fails to fulfil Huquq-ul-Ibad, his good deeds will be given to the wronged, or their sins placed into his record.

4️⃣ Patience & Tolerance

The believer knows that whatever hardships he faces will be rewarded by Allah ﷻ and will elevate his ranks. So he faces every difficulty with patience and composure.

5️⃣ Character Building

Belief in Akhirat produces excellent morals and builds character. By displaying good morals, the believer becomes a cause for promoting akhlaq-e-hasana in society.

6️⃣ Bravery & Self-Sacrifice

The believer knows the Master of life and death is Allah ﷻ alone. His life is a trust from Allah. So he does not hesitate to sacrifice it in Allah's path and gladly attains Shahadat.

Q2. Short Questions

(i) Briefly describe the teachings of the Quran about the belief in Akhirat. ▼

The Quran teaches that this worldly life is temporary and the life of the Hereafter is eternal. Allah ﷻ will raise all humans after death for reckoning and recompense (Surah Ar-Rum 30:27). The universe was not created in play but with purpose (Surah Ad-Dukhan 44:38–40). Every deed, good or bad, will be weighed on the Mizan with absolute justice. The righteous will enter Jannah, and the disbelievers will remain in Jahannam forever.

(ii) With what kinds of blessings will Muslims be rewarded in the Hereafter? ▼

Believers will be granted radiant faces (noor), the shade of the Arsh, water from Hawd-e-Kawthar (sweeter than honey, colder than ice, whiter than milk), their records in the right hand, easy crossing of Pul Siraat in the blink of an eye, and finally entry into Jannah — the eternal abode of bliss where they will receive the glad tidings of Allah's pleasure.

(iii) How has the Quran refuted the doubts of the polytheists regarding resurrection? ▼

The polytheists of Makkah doubted how humans could be revived after death and decay. The Quran responds that Allah who originated creation the first time can revive it again — and that is easier for Him (Surah Ar-Rum 30:27). It also establishes rationally that the heavens and earth were not created in vain (Surah Ad-Dukhan 44:38–40); therefore, a Day of Judgement must exist for justice to be served.

(iv) Write briefly about Hawd-e-Kawthar. ▼

Hawd-e-Kawthar is the pond of sweet water that Prophet Muhammad ﷺ will be granted in the plain of gathering. Its water flows from a river of Jannah called Kawthar. The water is sweeter than honey, colder than ice, and whiter than milk. Believers will drink from the blessed hands of the Prophet ﷺ, and by its blessing they will not feel thirst until entering Jannah. Disbelievers, polytheists, and apostates will be denied this water.

(v) What is meant by Shafa'at-e-Kubra? ▼

Shafa'at-e-Kubra (the Greatest Intercession) is exclusive to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. Its benefit extends to the whole of humanity because through it the severity of waiting for the reckoning will end and the reckoning itself will begin. This is also called Maqam-e-Mahmood. The Prophet ﷺ himself said: "Maqam-e-Mahmood is intercession." (Jami' Tirmidhi: 3148)

Q3. Detailed Question

Write a detailed note on the belief in Akhirat. ▼

Introduction: The word Akhirat means "that which comes afterwards." Belief in Akhirat means that this worldly life will one day end, and a new eternal life will begin, in which humans will be raised for reckoning and recompense.

Importance: Belief in Akhirat is one of the most fundamental articles of faith in Islam and, in fact, in every revealed religion. Without it, human life has no accountability and no final purpose.

Quranic Evidence: The Quran repeatedly addresses the doubts of the polytheists. Surah Ar-Rum 30:27 explains that reviving creation is easier for Allah than originating it. Surah Ad-Dukhan 44:38–40 affirms that the universe was not created in play but with truth and purpose.

Rational Proof: Human intellect accepts that good deeds deserve reward and bad deeds deserve punishment. If there were no life after death, the creation of man — the noblest of creatures — would be purposeless, which is impossible for an All-Wise Creator.

Stages of the Hereafter: Barzakh (intermediate life) → Soor & Hashr (gathering) → Mizan (weighing of deeds) → Hawd-e-Kawthar (the pond) → Pul Siraat (the bridge) → finally Jannah or Jahannam.

Shafa'at: Intercession on that Day is of two kinds — Shafa'at-e-Kubra (exclusive to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, the Maqam-e-Mahmood) and Shafa'at-e-Sughra (through Prophets, angels, scholars, martyrs, saliheen, the Quran, and fasting). All intercession occurs only by Allah's permission.

Effects on Life: Belief in Akhirat inclines a person towards good deeds, keeps him away from evil, builds respect for rights, cultivates patience, perfects character, and inspires bravery and self-sacrifice in Allah's path.

The belief in Akhirat transforms the believer's entire outlook on life — turning this transient world into a field of preparation for an eternal and just existence.
