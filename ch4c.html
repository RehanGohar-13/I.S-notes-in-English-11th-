Chapter 4(C): Ethics & Etiquette of Social Relations

Mu'asharat, neighbourly rights, non-Muslim relations, social media etiquette & the Prophetic model

🎯 Learning Outcomes

After studying this lesson, students will be able to:

  • Understand the meaning, concept, importance and virtue of social relations.
  • Learn bright examples of social etiquette from the Seerat-e-Nabawi ﷺ, the Ahl-e-Bait (RA) and the Sahaba (RA).
  • Understand the etiquette, limits and boundaries of social relations in daily life, including the use of social media.
  • Assess their own speech and actions in light of the Prophetic model of social relations.

What Are Social Relations?

Mu'asharat (Social Living): Living together in harmony. Ta'alluq (Relation): Connection, attachment and bond. Social Relations refer to the mutual bonds, interactions and coexistence among the various members of society.

In daily life, a person encounters many individuals. Treating all of them well, fulfilling their rights and duties, and showing good behaviour is a requirement of Islamic teachings.

🌍 A Natural Human Need

Social relations are a natural need of human life because Allah created humans as social beings. It is impossible for a person to live alone. Interacting with others and maintaining social bonds is the foundation of a successful social life.

  • ✅ Solves personal and collective problems.
  • ✅ Increases understanding and awareness.
  • ✅ Promotes mutual cooperation.
  • ✅ Creates a strong, cohesive society.
• • •

Islamic Foundations of Social Relations

Islam is the religion of love, brotherhood, sincerity, goodness, goodwill, sympathy and selflessness — and all of these are the foundations of strong social relations. The Prophet ﷺ commanded these qualities and gave glad tidings of reward for them.

🏗️ Key Steps Islam Took to Strengthen Social Relations

  • 🤝 Spreading Salam (greeting of peace) among people.
  • 🤝 Taking the initiative in building relationships.
  • 🤝 Declaring every Muslim a brother of every other Muslim.
  • 🤝 Defining mutual rights and commanding their fulfilment.
  • 🤝 Making family the basic unit of society — the foundation of all social relations.
  • 🤝 Emphasising maintaining kinship ties (Silah Rahmi) — whoever joins ties, Allah joins him; whoever breaks them is deprived of Allah's mercy.
  • 🤝 Strong emphasis on good treatment of neighbours.
"اللہ کی قسم! وہ ایمان والا نہیں، وہ ایمان والا نہیں، وہ ایمان والا نہیں جس کے شر سے اس کا پڑوسی محفوظ نہ ہو۔"
"By Allah, he is not a believer! By Allah, he is not a believer! By Allah, he is not a believer — whose neighbour is not safe from his evil."
— Sahih Bukhari: 6016
• • •

The Prophet's ﷺ Social Relations — A Living Model

Despite being the ruler of Madinah, the Prophet ﷺ maintained ideal social relations with all citizens and all classes of society. He ﷺ cared for neighbours' rights, visited the sick, attended funerals, and was always accessible to orphans and widows.

🕊️ Relations with Non-Muslims

The Prophet ﷺ had excellent relations not only with Muslims but also with the Jews of Madinah:

  • He ﷺ engaged in trade and business with them.
  • A Jewish boy used to serve the Prophet ﷺ and loved him. When the boy fell ill, the Prophet ﷺ personally visited his home to inquire about his health.
  • He ﷺ exchanged gifts with non-Muslims.
  • He ﷺ hosted non-Muslim guests when needed.

🇪🇹 The Abyssinian Ambassadors

When ambassadors from Abyssinia (Habasha) came to Madinah, the Prophet ﷺ personally served them with his own hands and said:

"When the Muslims migrated to Abyssinia, these people served my companions. Therefore, it is my duty to serve them in return."

🧹 The Mosque Cleaner

A person who used to clean the mosque passed away. The people performed his funeral prayer and buried him without informing the Prophet ﷺ. When the Prophet ﷺ asked about him and learned of his death and burial, he ﷺ expressed displeasure that he had not been informed. He ﷺ then went to the man's grave and prayed for him.

This shows that in Islam, no person is insignificant — even a humble mosque cleaner deserves the attention and prayers of the head of state.

🌾 Relations with Villagers

The Prophet ﷺ also exchanged gifts with villagers and Bedouins and encouraged the Sahaba (RA) to do the same. He ﷺ had friendly relations with many rural people.

😄 Hazrat Zahir bin Haram (RA)

Hazrat Zahir (RA) was a villager. Whenever he visited the Prophet ﷺ, he would bring gifts from the countryside. In return, the Prophet ﷺ would give him gifts when he departed. The Prophet ﷺ would joke with him affectionately and say:

"Zahir is our villager, and we are his city friends."

🤝 Etiquette of Greeting & Meeting

Social relations begin with Salam. The Prophet ﷺ commanded the spreading of Salam. His personal etiquette when meeting someone:

  • He ﷺ would shake hands and would not pull his hand away until the other person did first.
  • He ﷺ would not turn his face away until the other person turned theirs first.
— Jami' Tirmidhi: 2490

Following the Prophet's ﷺ training, the Sahaba (RA) and Ahl-e-Bait (RA) also treated people with excellent manners and maintained warm social relations. People would approach them without hesitation for their needs.

• • •

Vices That Destroy Social Relations

The Prophet ﷺ strictly forbade all actions that damage social bonds:

  • 🚫 Mocking others and making fun of them.
  • 🚫 Fault-finding and suspicion (Bad Gumaani).
  • 🚫 Lying, envy, slander and arrogance.
  • 🚫 Deception and fraud.
"جس نے دھوکا دیا وہ ہم میں سے نہیں"
"Whoever deceives is not one of us."
— Jami' Tirmidhi: 1315
"دوسروں کو حقیر سمجھنا اور حق بات نہ ماننا — یہ تکبر ہے۔"
"Considering others inferior and refusing to accept the truth — this is arrogance."
— Sahih Muslim: 265
"تعلق توڑنے والا جنت میں داخل نہیں ہوگا۔"
"The one who breaks ties will not enter Paradise."
— Sahih Bukhari: 5984

Instead, the Prophet ﷺ commanded goodwill (Khair Khwahi), sympathy (Hamdardi), sincerity (Ikhlas), goodness (Bhalai) and selflessness (Ithar) — because these strengthen social relations.

• • •

📱 Social Media & Islamic Ethics

In the modern era, social relations have taken on new forms and dimensions, especially through various social media platforms. We must keep Islamic moral teachings in mind in this context as well.

🚫 Prohibited on Social Media

  • 🚫 Attacking someone's honour or character assassination.
  • 🚫 Revealing someone's secrets.
  • 🚫 Sharing content about someone's private life.
  • 🚫 Spreading lies and false information.
  • 🚫 Sharing someone's photos without permission.
  • 🚫 Spreading evil and immorality among people.
  • 🚫 Sensational rumours and inciting unrest.
  • 🚫 Misleading news and fake information.
  • 🚫 Joining groups that promote immoral activities — this causes waste of time, intellectual deviation and anarchy.

A Muslim must not only avoid such acts but also stay away from people and groups that want to use them for their nefarious purposes.

✅ Positive Use of Social Media

  • ✅ Promoting goodness — social media can play a vital role in spreading virtue in society.
  • ✅ Reaching the entire world with your voice and ideas in seconds.
  • ✅ Learning modern sciences, technology and skills to serve the nation.
  • ✅ Promoting positive activities so that goodness and virtue flourish in society.

Question 2: Short Answers

(i) Explain the meaning and concept of social relations. ▼

Mu'asharat means living together in harmony, and Ta'alluq means connection, attachment and bond. Social relations refer to the mutual bonds, interactions and coexistence among the various members of society. In daily life, a person encounters many individuals — treating all of them well, fulfilling their rights and showing good behaviour is a requirement of Islamic teachings. Social relations are a natural human need because Allah created humans as social beings. They solve problems, increase understanding, promote cooperation and create a strong society.

(ii) Give one example of social relations from the Seerat-e-Tayyibah. ▼

A Jewish boy used to serve the Prophet ﷺ and loved him dearly. When the boy fell ill, the Prophet ﷺ — despite being the head of state of Madinah — personally went to his home to visit him and inquire about his health. This beautiful example shows that the Prophet's ﷺ social relations extended beyond the Muslim community to all members of society regardless of faith, and that he ﷺ valued every individual's well-being.

(iii) What kind of social relations did the Prophet ﷺ maintain with non-Muslims? ▼

The Prophet ﷺ maintained excellent and respectful relations with non-Muslims. He ﷺ engaged in trade with the Jews of Madinah, visited a sick Jewish boy at his home, exchanged gifts with non-Muslims, and hosted non-Muslim guests. When Abyssinian ambassadors came to Madinah, he ﷺ personally served them, saying: "They served my companions when the Muslims migrated to Abyssinia, so it is my duty to serve them." This demonstrates that Islamic social relations are based on humanity, gratitude and mutual respect, not religious boundaries alone.

(iv) What steps did Islam take to strengthen and expand social relations? ▼

Islam took several practical steps to strengthen social relations:

  1. Spreading Salam — the greeting of peace was commanded to be made common.
  2. Taking initiative in building relationships was encouraged.
  3. Every Muslim was declared a brother of every other Muslim.
  4. Mutual rights were defined and their fulfilment was commanded.
  5. The family was established as the basic unit of society.
  6. Maintaining kinship ties (Silah Rahmi) was emphasised — whoever joins ties, Allah joins him.
  7. Strong emphasis on good treatment of neighbours.
  8. Reward was promised for all these actions, providing the best foundation for social living.
(v) How does social media affect social relations? ▼

Social media has a dual impact on social relations:

Negative effects: It can be used to attack others' honour, reveal secrets, share private content without permission, spread lies and rumours, incite unrest, and join groups that promote immorality — all of which destroy social bonds and lead to intellectual deviation and anarchy.

Positive effects: When used correctly, social media can spread goodness and virtue, allow one's voice to reach the entire world in seconds, provide access to modern sciences and technology, and enable people to serve their nation. A Muslim must ensure positive use and avoid all harmful activities on these platforms.

Question 3: Detailed Answer

Write a comprehensive note on the importance of social relations and their ethics and etiquette. ▼

Introduction

Social relations (Mu'asharat) refer to the mutual bonds, interactions and coexistence among members of society. Allah created humans as social beings — living alone is impossible. Strong social relations solve problems, increase understanding, promote cooperation and build a cohesive society. Islam is the religion of love, brotherhood, sincerity and selflessness, and all of these are the foundations of strong social relations.

1. Islamic Foundations

Islam took practical steps to strengthen social bonds: spreading Salam, taking initiative in relationships, declaring Muslims brothers, defining mutual rights, making family the basic unit, emphasising kinship ties and commanding good treatment of neighbours. The Prophet ﷺ said: "By Allah, he is not a believer whose neighbour is not safe from his evil" (Bukhari: 6016).

2. The Prophetic Model

Despite being the ruler of Madinah, the Prophet ﷺ maintained ideal relations with all classes:

  • He ﷺ visited the sick, attended funerals and was accessible to orphans and widows.
  • He ﷺ had trade relations with the Jews and visited a sick Jewish boy.
  • He ﷺ personally served Abyssinian ambassadors out of gratitude.
  • He ﷺ prayed at the grave of a humble mosque cleaner when people buried him without informing him.
  • He ﷺ exchanged gifts with villagers like Hazrat Zahir bin Haram (RA) and joked with him affectionately.
  • His handshake etiquette: he ﷺ would not pull his hand away or turn his face until the other person did first (Tirmidhi: 2490).

3. Vices That Destroy Social Relations

The Prophet ﷺ forbade mocking, fault-finding, suspicion, lying, envy, slander, arrogance and deception. He ﷺ said: "Whoever deceives is not one of us" (Tirmidhi: 1315) and "The one who breaks ties will not enter Paradise" (Bukhari: 5984). Instead, he ﷺ commanded goodwill, sympathy, sincerity and selflessness.

4. Social Media Etiquette

In the modern era, social media has created new dimensions of social relations. Islam requires us to avoid character assassination, revealing secrets, sharing private content, spreading rumours and joining immoral groups. Instead, we should use social media positively to spread goodness, share knowledge, learn modern skills and serve the nation.

Conclusion

The Prophet's ﷺ life is the perfect model of social relations. He ﷺ treated everyone — Muslims and non-Muslims, rich and poor, city dwellers and villagers — with equal respect, kindness and dignity. By following his ﷺ example, we can build a society based on love, mutual respect and cooperation.
