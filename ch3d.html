Chapter 3(D): Economic Teachings of the Prophet ﷺ

Islamic economic principles, lawful livelihood, prohibition of exploitation & circulation of wealth

🎯 Learning Outcomes

After studying this lesson, students will be able to:

  • Understand the economic principles of Islam and their importance in light of the Seerat-e-Nabawi ﷺ.
  • Know the importance and virtue of various means of lawful earning — trade, agriculture and labour — in light of the Seerat.
  • Understand the condemnation of concentration of wealth, hoarding, elimination of interest, avoidance of adulteration and dishonesty in light of the Prophet's ﷺ economic teachings.
  • Review the benefits and fruits of acting upon the Prophet's ﷺ economic teachings.
  • Learn from the examples of the Seerat and practically demonstrate the Prophet's ﷺ economic teachings.

Islamic Economic Principles

Just as Islam provides guidance in moral, social and spiritual matters, it also lays down fundamental economic principles and laws. The blessed life of the Prophet ﷺ covers all aspects of the economy.

🎯 Core Objective

The fundamental purpose of the economic system established by the Prophet ﷺ is human welfare and well-being (Falah-o-Behbood).

📜 Foundational Principles

  1. Allah is the Provider (Raziq) of the entire universe — the most basic economic principle.
  2. Every member of society has the right to choose any lawful economic activity and earn a livelihood according to their ability and capability.
  3. However, it is essential to observe the limits set by Shariah in all economic activities.
  4. The Prophet ﷺ forbade the exploitation of others for the acquisition of wealth.
  5. He ﷺ completely rejected interest (Soud), hoarding (Zakhira Andozi), speculation (Satta Baazi), gambling (Jua) and bribery (Rishwat).

⚖️ A Balanced System

The Islamic economic system is based on moral values. It neither teaches monasticism (renouncing the world) nor promotes a purely materialistic mindset where a person becomes so absorbed in earning wealth that he forgets the Hereafter.

The Prophet ﷺ established an economic system founded on:

  • Taqwa (God-consciousness)
  • Ithar (selflessness)
  • Sakhawat (generosity)
  • Khair Khwahi (goodwill)
  • Hamdardi (sympathy)
  • Gardish-e-Daulat (circulation of wealth)
  • Infaq fi Sabeelillah (spending in Allah's path)
  • Adl (justice) and Ihsan (excellence)
  • Cooperation in righteous deeds

🔄 Mechanisms for Circulation of Wealth

To prevent the concentration of wealth in a few hands, Islam prescribes multiple channels of wealth distribution:

Zakat • Khums • Fitrana • Sadaqah & Khairat • Inheritance distribution • Distribution of spoils of war (Ghanimah) • Fai • Qurbani

By implementing these, an ideal welfare society can be established.

Lawful Sources of Livelihood (Zara'i-e-Ma'ash)

1. Trade (Tijarat)

The Prophet ﷺ not only practised trade himself but also encouraged his Sahaba (RA) and the Ummah to do so.

"سچا اور دیانت دار تاجر قیامت کے دن انبیائے کرام، صدیقین اور شہداء کے ساتھ ہو گا۔"
"The truthful and honest merchant will be with the Prophets, the Truthful and the Martyrs on the Day of Judgment."
— Jami' Tirmidhi: 1209

Example from the Seerat: Before the announcement of Prophethood, the Prophet ﷺ took Sayyidah Khadijah's (RA) trade goods on a journey. Due to his ﷺ honesty and integrity, Sayyidah Khadijah (RA) earned extraordinary profits.

2. Agriculture (Zara'at)

Agriculture is also a lawful source of earning. The majority of the Ansar of Madinah were engaged in farming. Islam encourages cultivation and planting fruit-bearing trees.

"جو مسلمان کوئی درخت لگاتا ہے یا کوئی کھیت اگاتا ہے اس سے کوئی پرندہ، انسان یا جانور کھاتا تو وہ اس کا صدقہ ہو جاتا ہے حتیٰ اس کے لیے اجر و ثواب کا باعث بنتا ہے۔"
"Whatever a Muslim plants — a tree or a crop — from which a bird, human or animal eats, it counts as Sadaqah for him and becomes a source of reward."
— Sahih Bukhari: 2320

3. Craftsmanship & Labour (Hunrmandi)

A person who does not have capital for trade can earn a lawful livelihood through skill and craftsmanship. The Prophet ﷺ declared that earning through one's own hands is the best earning.

  • Dawood (AS) used to make armour with his own hands.
  • Zakariya (AS) worked as a carpenter.

Earning through lawful means not only provides halal sustenance but also fulfils the needs of society. Thus, craftsmanship contributes to both individual growth and collective welfare.

Prohibition of Unlawful Sources

The most prominent principle of the economic system given by the Prophet ﷺ is avoiding unlawful means of earning.

🚫 Concentration of Wealth (Irtikaz-e-Daulat)

Irtikaz-e-Daulat: Wealth being concentrated in the hands of a specific class or a few individuals, while its benefits do not reach the rest of society.

The Prophet ﷺ condemned the concentration of wealth and commanded its avoidance. The Quran warns of painful punishment for those who hoard wealth and deprive society of its benefits:

وَالَّذِيْنَ يَكْنِزُوْنَ الذَّهَبَ وَالْفِضَّةَ وَلَا يُنْفِقُوْنَهَا فِيْ سَبِيْلِ اللّٰهِ ۙ فَبَشِّرْهُمْ بِعَذَابٍ اَلِيْمٍ
"And those who hoard gold and silver and do not spend it in the way of Allah — give them tidings of a painful punishment."
— Surah At-Tawbah 9:34

🚫 Hoarding (Zakhira Andozi)

Zakhira Andozi: Stockpiling goods or essential items to sell them at inflated prices.

The Prophet ﷺ cursed the person who engages in hoarding.

🚫 Interest (Soud)

The Quran explicitly declares interest Haram and calls it an open war against Allah and His Messenger ﷺ.

  • The interest-based system increases inequality and economic oppression in society.
  • Interest makes the rich richer and the poor poorer.
  • It was prohibited to save humanity from economic exploitation.

🚫 Adulteration (Milawat) & Dishonesty (Khiyanat)

The Prophet ﷺ also forbade adulteration and dishonesty:

  • Adulteration is a form of deception that harms the buyer. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever adulterates is not one of us."
  • Dishonesty was condemned; the Prophet ﷺ declared the religion of a dishonest person unreliable and called dishonesty a sign of hypocrisy (Nifaq).

Benefits of Following the Prophet's ﷺ Economic Teachings

  • ✅ Poverty and exploitation are eliminated from society.
  • ✅ People's economic rights are protected.
  • ✅ Fair distribution of wealth becomes possible.
  • ✅ The poor class gains economic security.
  • ✅ The gap between rich and poor is reduced.
  • ✅ Transparency in business increases.
  • ✅ Prosperity and peace replace corruption in society.

Question 2: Short Answers

(i) State any two principles of the Islamic economic system. ▼
  1. Allah is the Provider (Raziq): The most fundamental principle is that the entire universe's sustenance comes from Allah alone. Every person has the right to earn through lawful means according to their ability, while observing the limits of Shariah.
  2. Circulation of Wealth (Gardish-e-Daulat): Wealth must not remain concentrated in a few hands. Islam prescribes mechanisms like Zakat, Khums, Fitrana, Sadaqah, inheritance and Qurbani to ensure wealth circulates throughout all levels of society, preventing Irtikaz-e-Daulat.
(ii) Write the Islamic teachings regarding agriculture. ▼

Islam considers agriculture a lawful and blessed source of livelihood. The majority of the Ansar of Madinah were farmers. The Prophet ﷺ encouraged cultivation and planting fruit-bearing trees. He ﷺ said: "Whatever a Muslim plants — a tree or a crop — from which a bird, human or animal eats, it counts as Sadaqah for him and becomes a source of reward" (Bukhari: 2320). This shows that agriculture is not merely an economic activity but also an act of ongoing charity (Sadaqah Jariyah).

(iii) Concentration of wealth causes distortion in the economic system — explain. ▼

Irtikaz-e-Daulat (concentration of wealth) means wealth being accumulated in the hands of a specific class or a few individuals while its benefits do not reach the rest of society. This causes economic distortion because:

  • The rich become richer and the poor become poorer, widening the gap between classes.
  • The majority of society is deprived of basic needs while a few enjoy excess.
  • It leads to economic exploitation and oppression.
  • It stifles economic activity as purchasing power concentrates in fewer hands.
  • The Quran warns of painful punishment for those who hoard gold and silver and do not spend in Allah's path (At-Tawbah 9:34).

Islam counters this through Zakat, Sadaqah, inheritance laws and other mechanisms that ensure wealth circulates to all levels of society.

(iv) How is the condemnation of interest expressed in Islam? ▼

Islam condemns interest (Soud) in the strongest possible terms:

  • The Quran explicitly declares interest Haram and calls it an open war against Allah and His Messenger ﷺ.
  • The interest-based system increases inequality and economic oppression — the rich become richer and the poor become poorer.
  • It was prohibited to save humanity from economic exploitation.
  • Islam promotes interest-free lending (Qarz-e-Hasana) and profit-sharing models as alternatives that ensure fairness and justice.
(v) Write two benefits of acting upon the economic teachings of Islam. ▼
  1. Elimination of poverty and exploitation: Through mechanisms like Zakat, Sadaqah and the prohibition of interest and hoarding, the economic teachings of Islam ensure that wealth reaches the poor and vulnerable, eliminating systemic exploitation.
  2. Reduction of the wealth gap: Fair distribution of wealth through inheritance laws, Khums, Fitrana and other channels reduces the gap between rich and poor, creating a more balanced and stable society where prosperity and peace replace corruption.

Question 3: Detailed Answer

Write a detailed note on the economic teachings of the Prophet ﷺ. ▼

Introduction

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ established a comprehensive economic system that covers all aspects of earning, spending, distribution and trade. Its fundamental purpose is human welfare (Falah-o-Behbood). The system is based on moral values — neither monasticism nor pure materialism.

1. Fundamental Principles

  • Allah is the Provider (Raziq) of the entire universe.
  • Every individual has the right to choose any lawful economic activity.
  • Shariah limits must be observed in all dealings.
  • Exploitation of others is strictly forbidden.
  • The system is built on Taqwa, Ithar, generosity, goodwill, sympathy, justice, Ihsan and cooperation.

2. Lawful Sources of Livelihood

Trade (Tijarat): The Prophet ﷺ practised trade himself and declared: "The truthful and honest merchant will be with the Prophets, the Truthful and the Martyrs on the Day of Judgment" (Tirmidhi: 1209). His honesty in Khadijah's (RA) trade earned extraordinary profits.

Agriculture (Zara'at): The Ansar of Madinah were mostly farmers. The Prophet ﷺ said planting a tree or crop from which any creature eats counts as Sadaqah (Bukhari: 2320).

Craftsmanship (Hunrmandi): The Prophet ﷺ declared earning by one's own hands as the best earning. Dawood (AS) made armour; Zakariya (AS) was a carpenter.

3. Prohibition of Unlawful Sources

Concentration of Wealth: The Quran warns of painful punishment for those who hoard gold and silver (At-Tawbah 9:34).

Hoarding (Zakhira Andozi): Stockpiling essentials to sell at inflated prices — the Prophet ﷺ cursed the hoarder.

Interest (Soud): Declared an open war against Allah and His Messenger ﷺ. It increases inequality — the rich get richer, the poor get poorer.

Adulteration (Milawat): "Whoever adulterates is not one of us."

Dishonesty (Khiyanat): A sign of hypocrisy; the religion of a dishonest person is unreliable.

Also prohibited: speculation, gambling and bribery.

4. Circulation of Wealth

To prevent concentration of wealth, Islam prescribes: Zakat, Khums, Fitrana, Sadaqah, inheritance distribution, spoils of war, Fai and Qurbani. These mechanisms ensure wealth flows to all levels of society.

5. Benefits

  • Elimination of poverty and exploitation.
  • Protection of economic rights.
  • Fair distribution of wealth.
  • Economic security for the poor.
  • Reduced gap between rich and poor.
  • Transparency in business.
  • Prosperity and peace replacing corruption.

Conclusion

The Prophet's ﷺ economic teachings provide a complete, balanced and just system that ensures individual prosperity alongside collective welfare. By implementing these principles, an ideal welfare society can be established — free from exploitation, inequality and corruption.
