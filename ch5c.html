Chapter 5(C): Islamic Teachings of Marriage & Divorce

Nikah, prohibited relations, Mehr, Walima, Talaq, Khula, Iddah & avoiding un-Islamic customs

🎯 Learning Outcomes

After studying this lesson, students will be able to:

  • Understand Nikah and its rules and issues (proposal, proposal criteria, Ijab-o-Qabool, Khutbah, witnesses, Haq Mehr, and Walima).
  • Understand the rulings and issues regarding Divorce (Talaq), Khula, Judicial Dissolution (Tanseekh-e-Nikah), and Iddah.
  • Recognize non-Islamic customs, extravagances, and useless expenditures in weddings in daily life.
  • Assess the practical benefits of Nikah and the individual and social impacts of divorce.
  • Lead a successful family life by understanding Shariah rulings on marriage and divorce.
  • Avoid un-Islamic wedding customs and understand the harms of extravagant celebrations.

Meaning & Concept of Nikah

Nikah (Marriage): A solemn social contract (Umrani Mu'ahada) between a man and a woman through which they begin their marital and family life.

Nikah is essential for the preservation of the human race, the continuation of humanity, and the stability of social life.

فَانْكِحُوْا مَا طَابَ لَكُمْ مِّنَ النِّسَآءِ
"So marry women of your choice..."
— Surah An-Nisa 4:3
"اے نوجوانوں کی جماعت! تم میں جسے بھی نکاح کرنے کے لیے مالی طاقت (حق مہر) ہو اسے نکاح کر لینا چاہیے کیوں کہ یہ نظر کو نیچی رکھنے والا اور شرمگاہ کی حفاظت کرنے والا عمل ہے اور جو کوئی غربت کی وجہ سے نکاح کی طاقت نہ رکھتا ہو، اسے چاہیے کہ روزے رکھے کیوں کہ روزہ اس کی خواہشاتِ نفسانی کو توڑ دے گا۔"
"O young men! Whoever among you has the financial capacity (to pay Mehr and support a wife), let him marry, for it lowers the gaze and guards chastity. And whoever is unable, let him fast, for fasting acts as a shield against carnal desires."
— Sahih Bukhari: 5066

Prohibited Relations in Marriage (Muharramat-e-Nikah)

Muharramat: Women with whom marriage is strictly forbidden in Shariah. Prohibitions fall into four distinct categories:

1. Hurmat-e-Nasab (Consanguinity)

Prohibited by blood relations:

  • Mother (and grandmothers)
  • Daughter (and granddaughters)
  • Sister (full, paternal, or maternal)
  • Paternal Aunt (Phuphi)
  • Maternal Aunt (Khala)
  • Niece — Brother's daughter (Bhatiji)
  • Niece — Sister's daughter (Bhanji)

2. Hurmat-e-Raza'at (Fosterage)

Prohibited by foster suckling relations (breastfed in infancy):

  • Foster Mother
  • Foster Daughter
  • Foster Sister
  • Foster Paternal Aunt
  • Foster Maternal Aunt
  • Foster Niece (Bhatiji / Bhanji)

3. Hurmat-e-Musaharat (Affinity / In-Laws)

Prohibited through marital relationships:

  • Wife's Mother (Mother-in-law / Saas)
  • Wife's daughter from a previous husband (Stepdaughter / Rabiya)
  • Son's Wife (Daughter-in-law / Bahu)
  • Father's Wife (Stepmother)

4. Jam' Bain al-Muharam (Uniting Prohibited Relations)

Simultaneously combining two close relatives in marriage:

  • Two sisters at the same time
  • A woman and her paternal aunt (Phuphi)
  • A woman and her maternal aunt (Khala)

Criteria for Selecting a Spouse

"عورت سے شادی چار چیزیں دیکھ کر کی جاتی ہے: اس کا مال دیکھ کر، اس کی خاندانی وجاہت دیکھ کر، اس کی خوب صورتی دیکھ کر اور اس کا دین دیکھ کر، تو تم دین دار عورت کو پانے کی کوشش کرو۔"
"A woman is married for four reasons: her wealth, her family status, her beauty, and her religion. So choose the one who is religious, may your hands be rubbed with dust!"
— Sunan Nasai: 3232

Engagement (Mangni / Khitbah)

Mangni (Khitbah): A formal proposal of marriage. The Quran terms the marriage proposal as "Khitbah".
وَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ فِيْمَا عَرَّضْتُمْ بِهٖ مِنْ خِطْبَةِ النِّسَآءِ
"And there is no blame upon you if you hint a proposal of marriage to women..."
— Surah Al-Baqarah 2:235
"کوئی شخص اپنے بھائی کی منگنی پر منگنی کا پیغام نہ بھیجے اور نہ ہی کوئی عورت اپنی بہن (سوتن) کو طلاق دینے کا مطالبہ کرے۔"
"No man should send a proposal over his brother's proposal, nor should a woman demand the divorce of her sister (co-wife)."
— Sahih Muslim: 3442

⚠️ Avoiding Unnecessary Delays

Creating self-imposed, artificial standards for marriage delays unions for young men and women. This leads to inferiority complexes, emotional distress, and moral risks. Islam strongly discourages delaying marriage once a suitable match is found.

Essentials of Nikah

1. Proposal & Acceptance (Ijab-o-Qabool)

Offer and acceptance are Fard (obligatory) for a valid Muslim marriage:

  • Ijab (Offer): Offering the marriage proposal with complete identification of the prospective spouse.
  • Qabool (Acceptance): Acceptance of the proposal by the other party.
  • Witnesses: Must take place in the presence of two sane, adult male witnesses (or one male and two females).
  • Guardian (Wali): Consent of the legal guardian is required according to Shariah rules.

2. Sermon of Marriage (Khutbah Nikah)

Reciting the Khutbah Nikah — praising Allah, sending blessings upon Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, and reciting Quranic verses of Taqwa and counsel — is a Sunnah.

3. Dower (Haq Mehr)

Haq Mehr: The mandatory financial right of the bride given by the groom at the time of marriage. Payment is Wajib (obligatory).
وَ اٰتُوا النِّسَآءَ صَدُقٰتِهِنَّ نِحْلَةً
"And give the women their dower graciously as a free gift..."
— Surah An-Nisa 4:4

⚖️ Types of Haq Mehr

  • Mehr-e-Mu'ajjal (Prompt Dower): Dower paid immediately at the time of Nikah.
  • Mehr-e-Mu'ajjal (Deferred Dower): Dower whose payment is delayed to an agreed future date or upon demand.

4. Marriage Feast (Walima)

Walima is a Sunnah feast hosted by the groom and his family to celebrate the marriage. Accepting a Walima invitation is mandatory for invited Muslims. However, a Walima feast where only the wealthy are invited and the poor are excluded is condemned in Hadith as the worst feast.

• • •

Dissolution of Marriage: Talaq, Khula & Tanseekh

📜 Talaq (Divorce)

The husband releasing his wife from the bond of Nikah using specific terms.

⚖️ Khula

Dissolution of marriage initiated by the wife by mutual consent, usually in exchange for returning Mehr or financial compensation.

🏛️ Tanseekh-e-Nikah

Judicial dissolution of Nikah by a court when a husband refuses Talaq or Khula and cruelty/abandonment is legally proven.

فَاِنْ خِفْتُمْ اَلَّا يُقِيْمَا حُدُوْدَ اللّٰهِ ۙ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِمَا فِيْمَا افْتَدَتْ بِهٖ
"And if you fear that they will not keep within the limits of Allah, there is no blame upon either of them concerning that by which she ransoms herself."
— Surah Al-Baqarah 2:229
"اللہ تعالیٰ کے نزدیک حلال چیزوں میں سب سے زیادہ ناپسندیدہ چیز طلاق ہے۔"
"The most detestable of lawful things in the sight of Allah is divorce."
— Sunan Abu Dawood: 2177
• • •

Types of Talaq

  • Talaq-e-Raj'i (Revocable Divorce): A single or double divorce after which the husband can reconcile (Rajoo) with his wife during the Iddah period without a new Nikah.
  • Talaq-e-Ba'in (Irrevocable Divorce): Immediately dissolves the Nikah. Reconciliation during or after Iddah requires a new Nikah (Tajdeed-e-Nikah) with a fresh dower. Khula and judicial dissolution (Tanseekh) also result in Talaq-e-Ba'in.
  • Talaq-e-Mughallazah (Severe/Triple Divorce): The most severe form. The marriage is completely dissolved, and direct remarriage between the former spouses is not permissible unless specific Shariah conditions (Halala) occur naturally.
• • •

Waiting Period (Iddah) & Its Rules

Iddah: The mandatory waiting period a woman must observe following the death of her husband, divorce, Khula, or judicial annulment before she can remarry.

⏳ Duration of Iddah

  • Death of Husband (Iddah-e-Wafat): 4 months and 10 days.
  • Divorced Woman (Iddah-e-Talaq): 3 menstrual cycles (3 Haidh).
  • Pregnant Woman (Hamilah): Until delivery / childbirth (Waza-e-Haml).
• • •

Eliminating Un-Islamic Customs & Extravagance

Islam made Nikah simple, but modern cultural practices have burdened it with extravagant customs, making it a financial mountain for poor and middle-class families.

"سب سے زیادہ برکت والا نکاح وہ ہے جس میں مشقت کم سے کم ہو۔"
"The most blessed marriage is the one with the least financial burden and difficulty."
— Musnad Ahmad: 23388

🚫 Prohibited & Un-Islamic Practices

  • 🚫 Loud vulgar music, dancing, free mixing of men and women, and unveiling.
  • 🚫 Un-Islamic rituals like extravagant Mehndi functions.
  • 🚫 Demanding dowry (Jahez) from the bride's family or lavish public displays of dowry.
  • 🚫 Taking heavy loans to host opulent Walima feasts. Extravagance and ostentation (Riya) are strictly forbidden.
• • •

Benefits of Nikah & Social Harms of Divorce

🌟 Benefits of Nikah

  • ✅ Promotes Taqwa, chastity, and protection from sins.
  • ✅ Brings blessings (Barakah) in sustenance (Rizq).
  • ✅ Guarantees the continuation of humanity and builds strong families.
  • ✅ Instills deep emotional peace, love, and mercy between spouses.
— Surah Ar-Rum 30:21

💔 Social Harms of Divorce

  • ❌ Shatters the family unit and damages the entire social fabric.
  • ❌ Severely traumatizes children psychologically and emotionally.
  • ❌ Deprives children of the balanced care of both mother and father.
  • ❌ Breeds long-term enmity and hatred between families.

Question 2: Short Answers

(i) Briefly state the importance of Nikah. ▼

Nikah is a sacred social contract that forms the foundation of human society. It safeguards chastity, protects from sins, brings blessing in sustenance (Rizq), provides emotional peace and mutual love, and ensures the continuation and preservation of the human race in a pure, lawful manner.

(ii) Explain the concept of Khula. ▼

Khula is the dissolution of marriage initiated by the wife through mutual agreement with her husband, usually in exchange for returning her dower (Mehr) or giving financial compensation. If the husband refuses, she may seek judicial dissolution (Tanseekh) through a Shariah court.

(iii) Write the types of Iddah (waiting period). ▼
  1. Iddah of Death: 4 months and 10 days for a widow.
  2. Iddah of Divorce: 3 menstrual cycles (3 Haidh) for a divorced woman.
  3. Iddah of Pregnancy: Until childbirth for a pregnant woman.
(iv) Define Talaq-e-Ba'in. ▼

Talaq-e-Ba'in is an irrevocable divorce that immediately dissolves the Nikah. The husband cannot reconcile during or after Iddah without performing a new Nikah (Tajdeed-e-Nikah) with a fresh dower. Khula and judicial annulment (Tanseekh) also result in Talaq-e-Ba'in.

(v) How can non-Islamic customs in weddings be avoided? ▼

Non-Islamic customs can be eliminated by adhering strictly to the Sunnah — simplifying wedding ceremonies, avoiding heavy dowry demands, refraining from music, dancing, free mixing, and extravagant feasts, and spreading public awareness regarding the Prophetic teaching that the most blessed Nikah is the one with the least financial burden.

Question 3: Detailed Answer

Write a detailed note on Nikah and its rulings in light of the Quran and Sunnah. ▼

Introduction

Nikah is a sacred social contract in Islam that regulates human relationships, preserves lineage, and builds a moral family structure. The Prophet ﷺ declared Nikah to be his Sunnah and encouraged youth with financial capability to marry.

1. Essentials of Nikah

  • Ijab-o-Qabool: Proposal and acceptance in the presence of two sane, adult Muslim witnesses.
  • Haq Mehr: An obligatory financial dower given to the bride (Surah An-Nisa 4:4), either prompt (Mu'ajjal) or deferred (Mu'ajjal).
  • Walima: A Sunnah wedding feast hosted by the groom, emphasizing inclusion of the poor.

2. Prohibited Marriages (Muharramat)

Islam strictly forbids marriage within specific categories: consanguinity (blood relations like mothers, sisters, daughters), fosterage (foster mothers/sisters), affinity (in-laws like mothers-in-law), and combining two close relatives (like two sisters) simultaneously.

3. Dissolution of Marriage (Talaq & Khula)

While Islam preserves marriage, it permits divorce when reconciliation fails. Talaq is declared the most detested of permissible things to Allah (Abu Dawood: 2177). It includes Talaq-e-Raj'i (revocable), Talaq-e-Ba'in (irrevocable), and Talaq-e-Mughallazah. Women are granted the right of Khula or judicial dissolution (Tanseekh).

4. Iddah (Waiting Period)

Iddah ensures lineage clarity and emotional closure: 4 months 10 days for widows, 3 cycles for divorcees, and until delivery for pregnant women.

5. Simplifying Weddings

The Prophet ﷺ taught that the most blessed Nikah is the one with the least financial burden (Musnad Ahmad: 23388). Muslims must eradicate extravagant rituals, dowry demands, and un-Islamic celebrations.

Conclusion

Adhering to Islamic guidelines in Nikah and family life guarantees emotional tranquility, societal stability, and divine blessings.
