Chapter 5(B): Islamic Teachings of Inheritance

Wirasat, Wasiyyat, rights of women & orphans, and economic justice in Islam

🎯 Learning Outcomes

After studying this lesson, students will be able to:

  • Understand the literal and technical meaning and concept of inheritance (Wirasat).
  • Gain familiarity with the rules and principles of inheritance and bequest (Wasiyyat) in light of the Quran and Sunnah.
  • Know the severe Quranic warnings for those who deprive rightful heirs of their inheritance.
  • Understand the importance of distributing inheritance and act upon Islamic teachings in practical life.
  • Understand the sensitivity regarding the inheritance rights of women and orphans and implement Shariah rules.
  • Evaluate the social and economic benefits of distributing inheritance and the harms of withholding it.

Meaning & Concept of Inheritance (Wirasat)

Literal Meaning: The transfer of something from one person to another.
Technical Definition (Islamic Law): The property, wealth, or assets (Tarkah) transferred from the ownership of a deceased person to their living rightful heirs according to Shariah rules.

The Quran promises glad tidings of Paradise for those who fulfill Allah's commands regarding the distribution of inheritance, and warns of painful punishment in Hellfire for those who violate these laws.

Usurping inheritance is classified in the Quran as disobedience to Allah and His Messenger ﷺ and a characteristic of disbelievers:

وَتَأْكُلُوْنَ التُّرَاثَ اَكْلًا لَّمًّا ۙ وَتُحِبُّوْنَ الْمَالَ حُبًّا جَمًّا
"And you consume inheritance, devouring it altogether, and you love wealth with immense love."
— Surah Al-Fajr 89:19-20

🏛️ State Responsibility & Rights of People

Withholding inheritance is a direct violation of Allah's commands and a grave breach of Huqooq-ul-Ibad (Rights of People). Under Islamic governance, distributing an estate (Tarkah) according to Shariah is a primary responsibility of the state, and any government that neglects this duty fails in its fundamental obligation.

• • •

Pre-Islamic Era vs. Islamic Revolution

❌ Pre-Islamic Era (Jahiliyyah)

  • Women and children were completely excluded from inheritance.
  • The strong and powerful seized the entire estate, usurping the shares of vulnerable relatives.
  • Other religions and cultures lacked moderation and justice — some excluded daughters entirely or favored certain sons unfairly over others.

✨ The Islamic Revolution

  • Islam established clear, fixed, and equitable shares for both men and women.
  • Women, children, parents, and spouses are all guaranteed mandatory shares.
  • It eliminated favoritism, ensuring justice regardless of physical strength, gender, or age.
• • •

Prerequisites & Rules Before Inheritance Distribution

📋 Mandatory Order of Estate Settlement

Before any estate (Tarkah) is distributed among heirs, three mandatory steps must be executed in strict sequence:

  1. Burial & Funeral Expenses (Tadfeen): Funeral costs for the deceased according to reasonable Shariah standards.
  2. Payment of Debts (Qarz): Settling all outstanding debts owed by the deceased to individuals or institutions.
  3. Execution of Bequest (Wasiyyat): Fulfilling any valid bequest made by the deceased (up to 1/3 of the remaining estate).

⚠️ Rules Regarding Charity Prior to Distribution

It is strictly prohibited to give voluntary charity or Sadaqah from the deceased's estate before formal distribution, unless all adult heirs explicitly give permission. If any heir is a minor (non-baligh), giving charity from the estate is completely forbidden under any circumstances to protect the minor's property.

⚖️ Science of Inheritance (Ilm-ul-Meerath)

Distributing inheritance according to the Quran is a Fard-e-Ain (individual obligation) upon the heirs. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ described the knowledge of inheritance as "half of all knowledge" (Nisf-ul-Ilm) because it deals with half of human existence — death and estate distribution.

Specific shares vary dynamically depending on the surviving family members (children, spouse, parents, siblings).

• • •

Bequest (Wasiyyat) & Its Shariah Rules

Wasiyyat (Bequest): A direction or instruction given by a person during their lifetime to transfer a portion of their wealth or property to a specific individual, religious institution, traveler's lodge, or orphanage after their death.

📜 Fundamental Shariah Rules of Bequest

  • ✍️ Documentation & Witnesses: To prevent future disputes, a Wasiyyat should be written in the presence of two trustworthy witnesses.
  • 🚫 No Bequest for Legal Heirs: A bequest cannot be made in favor of anyone who is already a legal heir under Shariah ("La Wasiyyata li-Waarith"). Heirs receive their fixed shares automatically.
  • 📊 One-Third (1/3) Limit: A bequest cannot exceed one-third (1/3) of the net estate (after paying funeral costs and debts).
  • ⚖️ Execution Boundary: Executing a valid bequest up to 1/3 is mandatory for heirs. Any amount specified beyond 1/3 is not legally binding on heirs unless all adult heirs voluntarily consent.

Benefits of Proper Inheritance Distribution

🌟 Spiritual & Personal Benefits

  • ✅ Attaining the pleasure of Allah and earning Paradise.
  • ✅ Ensuring wealth remains Halal, leading to the acceptance of financial acts of worship (Sadaqah, Hajj).
  • ✅ Demonstrating obedience to the fundamental commands of Shariah.

🌍 Social & Economic Benefits

  • ✅ Prevents the concentration of wealth (Irtikaz-e-Daulat) in a few hands.
  • ✅ Eliminates family disputes, litigation, and mutual hatred.
  • ✅ Fosters family unity, love, and selflessness (Ithar).
  • ✅ Helps eliminate poverty and hardship in society by redistributing resources.
• • •

Harms of Withholding Inheritance & Usurping Rights

Usurping inheritance, particularly that of orphans and women, invites severe divine anger and warnings in the Quran:

وَلْيَخْشَ الَّذِيْنَ لَوْ تَرَكُوْا مِنْ خَلْفِهِمْ ذُرِّيَّةً ضِعَافًا خَافُوْا عَلَيْهِمْ
"And let those [executors and guardians] fear [Allah] just as they would fear for their own helpless children if they were to leave them behind."
— Surah An-Nisa 4:9

This verse reminds guardians to treat orphans with the same empathy and protective care they would want for their own vulnerable children if they were to pass away. The Quran warns that those who devour the property of orphans unjustly consume nothing but fire into their bellies.

💥 Consequences of Unjust Distribution

  • ❌ Earns Allah's severe displeasure and anger.
  • ❌ The usurper fills their stomach with Hellfire.
  • ❌ Spreads bitterness, animosity, and hatred among family members.
  • ❌ Tramples upon justice and human rights (Huqooq-ul-Ibad).
  • ❌ Destroys the Islamic family system and breaks kinship ties (Silah Rahmi).
  • ❌ Ruins social peace, causing long-term legal battles and societal degradation.

Question 2: Short Answers

(i) Write the meaning and concept of inheritance (Wirasat). ▼

Literally, Wirasat (Inheritance) means the transfer of something from one person to another. In Islamic law, it refers to the property, wealth, and assets (Tarkah) transferred from a deceased person to their living rightful heirs according to Shariah rules. Distributing inheritance is an individual obligation (Fard-e-Ain) and a core pillar of economic justice in Islam.

(ii) Explain the meaning and concept of bequest (Wasiyyat). ▼

Wasiyyat (Bequest) refers to a direction or instruction given by a person during their lifetime regarding the disposal of a portion of their property after death — such as allocating funds to a charity, non-heir relative, orphanage, or religious cause. It takes effect only after the person's passing.

(iii) Write two Shariah rules governing a bequest (Wasiyyat). ▼
  1. No bequest for an heir: A bequest cannot be made in favor of anyone who is already a rightful heir under Shariah ("La Wasiyyata li-Waarith").
  2. One-third (1/3) limit: A bequest cannot exceed one-third of the net estate (after funeral costs and debts are settled). Any bequest beyond 1/3 is not legally binding on heirs unless all adult heirs voluntarily consent.
(iv) Write any two benefits of distributing inheritance. ▼
  1. Circulation of wealth: It prevents the concentration of wealth in a few hands and redistributes financial resources across generations and relatives.
  2. Family harmony and justice: Fair distribution eliminates envy, fights, and long-term litigation, fostering family love and social peace.
(v) How does unjust distribution of inheritance cause breakdown in the family system? ▼

Unjust distribution or usurpation of inheritance (especially depriving women and orphans) destroys mutual trust, breeds deep-seated hatred, and causes intense bitterness among relatives. It leads to the rupture of kinship ties (Silah Rahmi), prolonged legal battles, and the ultimate collapse of family unity, replacing love and selflessness with greed and oppression.

Question 3: Detailed Answer

Write a detailed note on the importance and rules of inheritance distribution in light of the Quran and Sunnah. ▼

Introduction

Inheritance (Wirasat) in Islam is a divine, mandatory system of wealth redistribution established directly by Allah in the Quran. Unlike pre-Islamic traditions that excluded women and minors, Islam established fixed, just, and inescapable shares for all rightful heirs.

1. Quranic Importance & Status

The Quran specifies inheritance shares in detail (notably in Surah An-Nisa). Allah promises Paradise for those who obey these laws and warns of humiliating punishment in Hellfire for violators. Consuming inheritance unjustly is described as devouring wealth greedy and sinful (Surah Al-Fajr 89:19-20).

2. Order of Estate Settlement

Before distributing any shares to heirs, three mandatory steps must be executed in order:

  1. Pay burial and funeral expenses (Tadfeen).
  2. Settle all outstanding debts (Qarz).
  3. Execute valid bequests (Wasiyyat) up to 1/3 of the net estate.

3. Rules of Bequest (Wasiyyat)

A bequest is limited to non-heirs and cannot exceed 1/3 of the remaining estate. It should be documented with two witnesses to prevent disputes.

4. Protection of Women & Orphans

Islam strictly protects the property of vulnerable heirs. Surah An-Nisa (4:9) warns guardians to fear Allah regarding orphans' property as if it were their own children's future. Usurping an orphan's or woman's inheritance fills one's belly with Hellfire.

5. Individual & Social Benefits

  • Economic Balance: Prevents wealth concentration (Irtikaz-e-Daulat) and alleviates poverty.
  • Spiritual Acceptance: Ensures personal wealth is Halal so that financial worships are accepted.
  • Social Peace: Eliminates court battles and maintains family kinship (Silah Rahmi).

Conclusion

The Islamic inheritance system is a cornerstone of socio-economic justice. Full compliance with these divine laws is obligatory for every Muslim to ensure peace in this life and salvation in the Hereafter.
