Prevention of Islamophobia & Our Responsibilities

Chapter 7(C) — اسلاموفوبیا اور ہماری ذمہ داریاں

بِسْمِ اللهِ الرَّحْمٰنِ الرَّحِيْمِ "In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful."

🎯 Learning Outcomes

After studying this lesson, students will be able to:

  1. Understand the literal and contextual meaning of the term Islamophobia.
  2. Analyze the various forms, manifestations, and global impacts of Islamophobia.
  3. Comprehend the significance of unity among Muslims in light of the national decree "Paigham-e-Pakistan".
  4. Realize the duties of Muslim youth in countering Islamophobia through constructive dialogue and respect for sacred symbols.
  5. Promote mutual understanding, tolerance, and respect among diverse faith communities.
  6. Identify negative media propaganda against Islam and play an active role in neutralizing misperceptions.

📖 Meaning and Concept of Islamophobia

The word Phobia literally means an irrational, persistent, and extreme fear of a specific object or situation.

Derived from this concept, Islamophobia refers to an irrational, baseless fear, prejudice, hatred, or hostility directed against Islam, its sacred symbols, and Muslims. It represents a structured narrative aimed at generating negative sentiments against Islamic civilization, culture, and values.

Evolution in the Post-9/11 Era

Following the events of September 11, 2001 (9/11), the term gained widespread currency in Western media. False narratives were propagated claiming that Islam promotes extremism, opposes freedom of expression, rejects peaceful co-existence, and denies basic human rights.

Furthermore, the sacred Islamic concept of Jihad was deliberately distorted and misrepresented as terrorism.

⚠️ Manifestations and Impacts of Islamophobia

Due to hostile anti-Islamic propaganda, Muslims in Western societies face severe systemic discrimination and physical violence:

  • Violation of Religious Rights: Banning Muslim women’s Hijab (headscarf) and religious attire in public spaces.
  • Attacks on Places of Worship: Vandalism, arson, and armed attacks against mosques.
  • Desecration of Sacred Texts: Public incidents of burning or defiling copies of the Holy Quran.
  • Attacks on Prophetic Honor: Blasphemous depictions, caricatures, and offensive publications targeted at the honor of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ.
Insulting the sacred sanctities of any religion is an international crime and a blatant violation of global peace, security, and human co-existence.
· · ·

🌐 Global Recognition and UN Resolutions

March 15: International Day

Following the March 15, 2019 terror attack on two mosques in Christchurch, New Zealand (where 51 Muslims were martyred), the UN designated March 15th as the International Day to Combat Islamophobia.

Pakistan's Landmark Resolution

On March 15, 2024, the UN General Assembly adopted a landmark resolution presented by Pakistan on behalf of the OIC to combat the rising wave of hatred against Muslims worldwide.

· · ·

📜 The "Paigham-e-Pakistan" Consensus Decree

Along with external propaganda, internal inter-sectarian conflicts and unauthorized armed actions by non-state actors gave pretexts to Islamophobic narratives. To eliminate violent extremism internally, the State of Pakistan issued a historic national consensus document titled "Paigham-e-Pakistan" in 2018.

  • Backed by a unanimous Fatwa signed by over 1,800 prominent Islamic scholars across all schools of thought.
  • Asserted that declaring Jihad is the sole prerogative of the Islamic State, not private individuals or armed groups.
  • Unequivocally condemned suicide bombings, armed insurgency, and attacks on innocent civilians as Haram (unlawful).
  • Presented the authentic, peaceful image of Islam grounded in the Quran and Sunnah.

🤝 Responsibilities of Modern Muslim Youth

  1. Wisdom over Emotionalism: Avoid reactive or violent outbursts when provoked; respond with intellectual depth, legal remedies, and moral dignity.
  2. Emulating Prophetic Tolerance: Emulate the diplomacy of the Prophet ﷺ, such as the Charter of Madinah (Misaq-e-Madinah), which established a peaceful multi-faith society.
  3. Protecting Minority Rights: Guarantee complete protection, dignity, and religious rights for non-Muslim minorities living in Muslim-majority societies.
  4. Constructive Social Media Advocacy: Utilize digital platforms effectively to counter anti-Islamic propaganda and highlight Prophetic ethics.
  5. Humanitarian Excellence: Lead international relief efforts and social service to demonstrate Islamic compassion in action.

Q2. Short Questions

(i) What is meant by Islamophobia? ▼

Islamophobia refers to an irrational, unfounded fear, prejudice, hatred, or hostility directed against Islam, Muslims, and Islamic sanctities. It involves a systematic narrative that falsely associates Islam with terrorism, intolerance, and extremism.

(ii) State two manifestations or forms of Islamophobia. ▼

1. Physical & Symbolic Attacks: Violent attacks on mosques, bans on Islamic attire/Hijab, and public desecration of the Holy Quran.
2. Blasphemy: Targeted publications, caricatures, and media narratives insulting the sacred personality of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ.

(iii) Analyze the role of "Paigham-e-Pakistan" in countering terrorism. ▼

"Paigham-e-Pakistan" (2018) is a unanimous national narrative backed by over 1,800 Islamic scholars. It counters terrorism by clarifying that declaring Jihad is the sole right of the Islamic State and declaring suicide attacks and violence against civilians completely forbidden (Haram).

(iv) Examine two challenges faced by Muslims living in Western societies. ▼

1. Social Discrimination & Hate Crimes: Facing verbal abuse, workplace discrimination, and physical violence due to Islamic identity.
2. Infringement on Religious Freedom: Restrictions on constructing mosques or practicing religious rituals openly without prejudice.

(v) State two responsibilities of modern Muslim youth to eliminate Islamophobia. ▼

1. Constructive Digital Advocacy: Utilizing social media wisely to present Islam’s peaceful teachings and rebut false propaganda.
2. Wisdom & Dialogue: Reacting with moral dignity, legal channels, and interfaith dialogue rather than emotional or violent outbursts.

Q3. Detailed Question

Write a comprehensive note on "Islamophobia and the Responsibilities of Muslims". ▼

Islamophobia is a major global challenge rooted in irrational fear, media misrepresentation, and political bias.

Key Manifestations: Quran desecration, blasphemous cartoons, armed attacks on mosques (e.g., Christchurch 2019), and banning of Islamic attire in public spaces.

Responsibilities of Muslims:
1. Diplomatic Action: Strengthening OIC and UN platforms (such as the UN designating March 15 as International Day to Combat Islamophobia).
2. Internal Reform: Implementing decrees like Paigham-e-Pakistan to eliminate extremism and deny foreign media pretext.
3. Emulating Prophetic Character: Following the Charter of Madinah to safeguard non-Muslim minority rights and engage in dialogue.
4. Digital Advocacy: Harnessing media and humanitarian work to showcase Islam’s timeless beauty and compassion.
