Chapter 2(D): Philosophy of Hajj & Qurbani

The fifth pillar of Islam — a universal gathering of unity, equality & sacrifice

🎯 Learning Outcomes

After studying this lesson, students will be able to:

  • Understand the obligation, wisdom and philosophy of Hajj.
  • Know the rulings and issues of Hajj (conditions, rites, types etc.).
  • Understand the virtues, rulings and issues of Qurbani (sacrifice).
  • Review the etiquettes of visiting Haramain Sharifain and the individual & collective impact of Hajj on practical life.
  • Become people who perform Hajj and Qurbani with proper etiquette when they have the means.
  • Live life according to the spirit of collectivity by understanding the philosophy of Hajj.

In the Islamic system of worship, along with Salah, Sawm and Zakat, Hajj is also a vital and comprehensive act of worship.

Literal meaning: "To intend / to resolve".
Technical meaning: The intention of a Sahib-e-Istita'at (capable) Muslim to visit the Bait-ullah during the days of Hajj to perform the rites of Hajj is called Hajj.
• • •

Obligation, Wisdom & Philosophy of Hajj

The command of Hajj's obligation was revealed in the 9th year of Hijrah. The Prophet ﷺ performed one Hajj and four Umrahs in his blessed life.

وَلِلّٰهِ عَلَي النَّاسِ حِجُّ الْبَيْتِ مَنِ اسْتَطَاعَ اِلَيْهِ سَبِيْلًا ۚ وَمَنْ كَفَرَ فَاِنَّ اللّٰهَ غَنِيٌّ عَنِ الْعٰلَمِيْنَ
"And Hajj (pilgrimage) to the House is a duty that mankind owes to Allah — for those who can afford the journey. And whoever disbelieves, then indeed Allah is free from need of the worlds."
— Surah Aal-e-Imran 3:97

The Prophet ﷺ said:

"The reward of Hajj-e-Mabroor (accepted Hajj) is nothing but Paradise. Whoever performs Hajj and does not utter any obscene or indecent word, nor commits any sin, all his previous sins will be forgiven."
— Jami' Tirmidhi: 933

🕋 A Comprehensive Act of Worship

Hajj is a unique worship that simultaneously combines spiritual, physical and financial acts of worship.

Rites (Manasik) of Hajj include:

  • Putting on Ihram
  • Going to Mina
  • Wuqoof-e-Arafat (standing at Arafat)
  • Staying at Muzdalifah
  • Throwing pebbles at Jamarat (Rami)
  • Qurbani (sacrifice)
  • Halq (shaving) or Qasr (trimming)
  • Tawaf and Sa'i
• • •

Conditions of Hajj

For Hajj to be obligatory on a man or woman, the following conditions must be met:

  1. Muslim — must be a Muslim.
  2. Baligh — must have reached puberty.
  3. Aaqil — must be sane.
  4. Healthy — must be physically fit.
  5. Financial capability — must own enough wealth that, besides the travel expenses of Hajj, there is also adequate money for the maintenance of the family at home.
  6. For men: wearing the Ihram — two unstitched white sheets — is also among the conditions.

Duration of Rites: The rites of Hajj begin on the 8th of Dhul-Hijjah (called Yawm-ut-Tarwiyah) and conclude on the 13th of Dhul-Hijjah.

Etiquettes: Avoiding all kinds of immoral behaviour, abusive language and quarrels. Certain lawful matters such as hunting and marital relations are also forbidden during Ihram.

• • •

Rulings & Issues (Ahkam-o-Masa'il)

📍 Meeqat

Anyone travelling to Mecca for Hajj from outside must enter the boundary of Haram only after putting on Ihram at the Meeqat.

Meeqat: The designated places in the four directions around the Ka'bah; it is forbidden to cross into the Haram boundary without wearing Ihram.

Pakistan's Meeqat: Yalamlam — located 128 km from the Ka'bah.

✈️ For Pilgrims from Pakistan

  • If going directly to Mecca: must put on Ihram before the plane enters the boundary of Meeqat — i.e. from home, from the airport, or on the plane before Meeqat.
  • If going to Madinah Munawwarah first: Ihram is not required.

Three Types of Hajj

1. Hajj-e-Ifrad

For residents of Mecca (already free of Ihram restrictions). On 8th Dhul-Hijjah they put on Ihram with the intention of Hajj only. The person is called Mufrid (one who performs Hajj alone).

2. Hajj-e-Tamattu'

Enters Ihram at Meeqat with the intention of performing Umrah first, removes Ihram after Umrah, then puts on Ihram again on 8th Dhul-Hijjah for Hajj rites.

3. Hajj-e-Qiran

Enters Ihram with the combined intention of both Umrah and Hajj together. The Prophet ﷺ performed Hajj-e-Qiran — and this is the most virtuous type.

• • •

Sequence of the Rites of Hajj

🌅 8th Dhul-Hijjah (Yawm-ut-Tarwiyah)

Pilgrims put on Ihram after Fajr prayer, offer two rak'ats of nafl, make the intention of Hajj, and recite the Talbiyah aloud:

لَبَّيْكَ، اَللّٰهُمَّ لَبَّيْكَ، لَبَّيْكَ لَا شَرِيْكَ لَكَ لَبَّيْكَ، اِنَّ الْحَمْدَ وَالنِّعْمَةَ لَكَ وَالْمُلْكَ، لَا شَرِيْكَ لَكَ
"Here I am, O Allah, here I am. Here I am, You have no partner, here I am. Indeed all praise, grace and sovereignty belong to You. You have no partner."

📅 Day-by-Day Sequence

  1. 8th Dhul-Hijjah: Ihram & Talbiyah → travel to Mina (a few km from Mecca).
  2. 9th Dhul-Hijjah (Fajr onwards): After Fajr, travel from Mina to the Plain of Arafat — perform Wuqoof-e-Arafah (the central pillar of Hajj — "standing in the plain of Arafat"). Listen to the Hajj sermon; combine Zuhr and Asr. At Maghrib time, leave for Muzdalifah without praying Maghrib.
  3. Night in Muzdalifah: Combine Maghrib and Isha. Collect pebbles for Rami. After Fajr, leave for Mina without waiting for sunrise.
  4. 10th Dhul-Hijjah (in Mina): Throw 7 pebbles at Jamrah Aqabah (the last of the three) → perform Qurbani → do Halq (shave) or Qasr (trim). Then remove Ihram, bathe, and wear everyday clothes.
  5. 11th, 12th (& 13th) Dhul-Hijjah: Stay 3 nights in Mina; each day throw 7 pebbles at each of the three Jamarat.
  6. Tawaf-e-Ziyarah: During these days, go to the Haram and perform Tawaf and Sa'i (between Safa & Marwah) — this completes the Hajj.
  7. If one cannot leave Mina by Maghrib of 12th Dhul-Hijjah, Rami must also be performed on 13th.

✂️ Halq vs Qasr

  • Halq: Shaving the entire head.
  • Qasr: Trimming the hair short.
• • •

Haramain Sharifain

Haramain = "the two honoured places" — Mecca Mukarramah and Madinah Munawwarah.

Those who go for Hajj and Umrah also visit Rawdah of the Prophet ﷺ in Madinah, present Durood-o-Salam, and offer nawafil in Masjid-e-Nabawi — especially in Riyadh-ul-Jannah.

Opposite Masjid-e-Nabawi lies the historical graveyard Baqi'-ul-Gharqad (commonly: Jannat-ul-Baqi) where the Ahl-ul-Bayt (RA), Sahaba Kiram (RA) and notable figures of Islam are buried; pilgrims visit these graves.

• • •

Hajj — Manifestation of Unity & Equality

  • Hajj is a manifestation of the unity and equality of the Muslim Ummah.
  • The distinction between rich and poor is erased.
  • Feelings of brotherhood, harmony and unity emerge among Muslims of the entire world.
  • An atmosphere of cooperation and coordination develops in the fields of mutual relations, politics, trade, defence, education and other sectors.
  • It is among the etiquettes of Hajj that all pilgrims avoid fighting, abusive language, mischief and immodesty.
• • •

Qurbani (Sacrifice)

Qurbani: Slaughtering a specific animal during the days of Eid-ul-Adha — in accordance with the Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ and his great forefather Prophet Ibrahim (AS) — for attaining the nearness (Qurb) of Allah.

On the day of Eid-ul-Adha, no act is more virtuous than Qurbani. This day is counted as the most excellent day of the year.

The Prophet ﷺ said regarding one who fails to perform Qurbani despite having the means:

"Whoever has the capacity to offer sacrifice but does not do so, let him not come near our Eidgah."
— Sunan Ibn Majah: 3123

📜 Rulings of Qurbani

  • Qurbani cannot be performed before Eid prayer.
  • It is most virtuous to slaughter with one's own hand.
  • If one cannot slaughter personally, another Muslim may do it on his behalf — but permission is essential.

💡 Philosophy of Qurbani

The philosophy of Qurbani is that one sacrifices his desires for the pleasure of Allah, and makes a pledge that if he must sacrifice even his life in the path of Allah, he will not hesitate.

Question 2: Short Answers

(i) State the obligation of Hajj. ▼

Hajj is the fifth pillar of Islam. Its obligation was revealed in the 9th year of Hijrah. Allah says:

وَلِلّٰهِ عَلَي النَّاسِ حِجُّ الْبَيْتِ مَنِ اسْتَطَاعَ اِلَيْهِ سَبِيْلًا "Hajj to the House is a duty mankind owes to Allah — for those who can afford the journey." — Aal-e-Imran 3:97

Hajj is obligatory once in a lifetime on every Muslim who is adult, sane, healthy and possesses the financial means (along with provision for his family). The Prophet ﷺ himself performed Hajj once in his lifetime.

(ii) Write any four rites of Hajj. ▼
  1. Putting on Ihram at the Meeqat with the intention of Hajj and reciting Talbiyah.
  2. Wuqoof-e-Arafat — standing in the plain of Arafat on 9th Dhul-Hijjah (central pillar).
  3. Rami al-Jamarat — throwing seven pebbles at Jamrah Aqabah on 10th, and all three Jamarat on 11th–12th.
  4. Tawaf-e-Ziyarah and Sa'i between Safa & Marwah to complete Hajj.
(iii) Write the conditions of Hajj. ▼
  1. Islam — must be a Muslim.
  2. Buloogh — must have reached puberty.
  3. Aql — must be sane.
  4. Health — must be physically capable of the journey.
  5. Financial means — must have wealth for travel expenses and enough left for family's maintenance.
  6. For men: wearing the Ihram (two unstitched white sheets).
(iv) State the philosophy of Qurbani. ▼

The philosophy of Qurbani is to sacrifice one's desires for the pleasure of Allah, following the Sunnah of Prophets Ibrahim (AS) and Muhammad ﷺ. It is a pledge that:

  • The believer places Allah's command above every personal love and attachment.
  • If the need arises, he will sacrifice even his life in the path of Allah without hesitation.
  • It revives the memory of Prophet Ibrahim's (AS) supreme obedience when he was ready to sacrifice his beloved son Isma'il (AS).
  • It teaches submission, obedience, gratitude and detachment from worldly attachments.
(v) How does Hajj become a means of promoting collectivity in the Muslim Ummah? ▼

Hajj is the greatest annual gathering of Muslims worldwide. It fosters collectivity in the following ways:

  • Equality: Rich and poor wear the same two white sheets of Ihram — the distinction of class, race and nationality disappears.
  • Brotherhood: Muslims from every corner of the globe meet, strengthening bonds of Islamic brotherhood (Ukhuwwah).
  • Unity: All pilgrims face one Qiblah, perform the same rites, and recite the same Talbiyah — a visual symbol of the Ummah's unity.
  • Cooperation: It becomes a platform for cooperation in politics, trade, defence, education and other sectors.
  • Discipline: It teaches collective discipline — moving together, standing together at Arafat, staying together at Mina & Muzdalifah.
  • Avoidance of discord: Fighting, abusive language, mischief and immodesty are prohibited — creating a peaceful collective environment.

Question 3: Detailed Answer

Write a comprehensive note on the rulings and issues of Hajj & Qurbani. ▼

Hajj — Rulings & Issues

Definition: Literally "intention"; technically, a capable Muslim's intention to visit the Bait-ullah during the days of Hajj to perform its prescribed rites.

Obligation: Revealed in the 9th year of Hijrah. Obligatory once in a lifetime on every capable Muslim (Aal-e-Imran 3:97). The Prophet ﷺ performed one Hajj and four Umrahs.

Conditions: Islam, puberty, sanity, physical health, financial capacity (travel + family maintenance), and for men — wearing Ihram.

Meeqat: The designated boundary points around Mecca; no one may enter the Haram without Ihram. Pakistan's Meeqat is Yalamlam, 128 km from the Ka'bah.

Three Types:

  • Hajj-e-Ifrad — only Hajj (for Mecca residents / Mufrid).
  • Hajj-e-Tamattu' — Umrah first, remove Ihram, then re-enter Ihram on 8th Dhul-Hijjah for Hajj.
  • Hajj-e-Qiran — single Ihram for both Umrah and Hajj. The Prophet ﷺ performed this and it is the most virtuous.

Rites (8th–13th Dhul-Hijjah):

  1. 8th Dhul-Hijjah — Ihram, Talbiyah, head to Mina.
  2. 9th — travel to Arafat, Wuqoof-e-Arafah (central pillar), Hajj sermon, combined Zuhr & Asr, then onward to Muzdalifah at Maghrib; combined Maghrib & Isha there; collect pebbles.
  3. 10th — Rami at Jamrah Aqabah (7 pebbles), Qurbani, Halq or Qasr, remove Ihram.
  4. 11th–12th (and possibly 13th) — Rami at all three Jamarat; stay nights in Mina.
  5. Tawaf-e-Ziyarah and Sa'i between Safa & Marwah complete the Hajj.

Etiquettes: Avoid obscenity, abuse, quarrels, mischief, immodesty, hunting, and marital relations during Ihram.

Visit to Madinah: Visiting the Rawdah of the Prophet ﷺ, presenting Durood-o-Salam, offering nawafil in Riyadh-ul-Jannah, and visiting Jannat-ul-Baqi.

Qurbani — Rulings & Issues

Definition: Slaughtering a specific animal in the days of Eid-ul-Adha, following the Sunnah of Prophets Ibrahim (AS) and Muhammad ﷺ, to attain nearness to Allah.

Virtue: No act on Eid-ul-Adha is more virtuous than Qurbani; it is the most excellent day of the year.

Warning: Whoever has the capacity but neglects Qurbani — "let him not come near our Eidgah" (Ibn Majah: 3123).

Rulings:

  • Cannot be done before Eid prayer.
  • Slaughtering with one's own hand is most virtuous.
  • If unable, another Muslim may slaughter on one's behalf — but permission is essential.

Philosophy: Sacrifice of personal desires for Allah's pleasure, and the pledge to lay down even one's life in Allah's path if needed — reviving Ibrahim's (AS) legacy of total submission.
